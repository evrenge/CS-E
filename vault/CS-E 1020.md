# CS-E 1020 — Engine Emissions

> [!summary]
> The engine type design must be shown to comply with the emission
> specifications of CS 34.2 in effect at the date of engine certification, by
> test or analysis or a combination of the two. The resulting data must be
> recorded. The specification fixes the means, the reference date and the
> recording duty. The emission limits are not in CS-E, and CS 34.2 refers
> onward to Part 21 and ICAO Annex 16.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Demonstrate, by test or analysis or combination thereof, that the engine type design complies with the emission specifications of CS 34.2 in effect at date of engine certification. | Required |
| — | Record the resulting data. | Required |

CS-E 1020 is a single unnumbered paragraph of two sentences, so the `Ref` cells
carry no sub-point identifier.

Three things are fixed here and nowhere else. The means is test, analysis, or a
combination — analysis alone is an accepted route. The reference version is
CS 34.2 "in effect at date of Engine certification", so a later amendment of
CS-34 does not retroactively apply to this type design. The data must be
recorded, which is a separate obligation from demonstrating compliance
[CS-E 1020].

[[AMC E 1020|AMC E 1020(2)]] draws the consequence of the reference date. Only
the type design at the time of certification is assessed against CS-34. The
standard applicable to a production engine depends on its date of manufacture.

## Compliance

- Emissions demonstration by test, analysis, or a combination, against CS 34.2 as in effect at the date of engine certification [CS-E 1020].
- Recorded emissions data from that demonstration [CS-E 1020].
- TCDS note in the format given in [[AMC E 1020|AMC E 1020(1)]], naming the CS-34 amendment complied with. [[CS-E 1000]] states that compliance with the specifications of Subpart F will be recorded in notes in the engine type certificate data sheet [CS-E 1000].
- Assessment of any further type design change for its effect on emissions characteristics and on CS-34 compliance [AMC E 1020(2)]. This is an accepted means, not a specification: CS-E 1020 addresses only the demonstration and recording at certification. Accepted means: [[AMC E 1020|AMC E 1020(2)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies, subject to [[CS-E 1000]]: compliance may be mandatory
depending on what CS-34 references.

CS 34.2 names no fuel, no operating point and no cycle. It is a single
sentence referring the applicant to point 21.A.21 of Part 21 [ext CS 34.2]. The
emission limits, the operating cycle and the measurement methods are in ICAO
Annex 16 Volume II: "The procedures for the evaluation of the emissions levels
are provided in the appendices to that volume" [ext GM1 21.B.85(a)]. The chain
is set out in [[CS 34.2]], and the vault stops at Annex 16.

[VERIFY: whether the declared fuel specifications of
[[CS-E 560|CS-E 560(a)(1)]] and the ratings of [[CS-E 40]] are inputs to the
emissions demonstration. The answer is in the Annex 16 Volume II appendices,
which this vault does not hold.]

The recording duty of CS-E 1020 and the continuing assessment in
[[AMC E 1020|AMC E 1020(2)]] outlast certification. Under that accepted means,
any later change to the type design is assessed for its effect on emissions
characteristics. Emissions therefore remain a live topic through the engine's
development life, rather than a one-off test.

Two questions remain open, and both are recorded in [[CS 34.2]] rather than
repeated here. The first is whether this paragraph reaches a turboshaft at all:
the Annex 16 part behind it is written for turbojet and turbofan engines. The
second is what the TCDS note may say, now that CS-34 is repealed and a reference
to it is not a record of compliance. Both questions are for the Agency, and
both are to be raised before the certification programme is agreed.
