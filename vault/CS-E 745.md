# CS-E 745 — Engine Acceleration

> [!summary]
> Two acceleration demonstrations are required on a test bed. The engine must
> reach rated Take-off Power when the power demand is raised from minimum test
> bed idle in not more than one second, under the most adverse permitted
> combination of bleed air and power extraction, without over-temperature, surge,
> stall or other detrimental effects. Separately, it must go from 15 % to 95 % of
> rated Take-off Power in not more than 5 seconds. The minimum response time is
> then measured under three defined load conditions.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(2)** | For rotorcraft engines, demonstrate on a test bed that the power increases to rated Take-off when the power demand is increased from minimum test bed idle to rated Take-off in not more than one second, with the appropriate adverse combination of bleed air and power extraction to be permitted in the aircraft, without over-temperature, surge, stall, or other detrimental factors occurring to the engine. | Required |
| **(a)(3)** | For all engines, demonstrate that an increase can be made from 15 % of the rated Take-off Power to 95 % of the rated Take-off Power in a time not greater than 5 seconds. | Required |
| **(a)(3)** | A longer time may be accepted if properly justified. | Permitted |
| **(a)(3)** | Achieve this power response from a stabilised static condition, using only the bleed air and accessories loads necessary to run the engine. | Required |
| **(b)** | Measure the minimum response time to 95 % of the rated Take-off Power resulting from increasing the power demand in not more than one second from the minimum test bed idle condition, starting from a stabilised condition, under the load conditions of (b)(1), (b)(2) and (b)(3). | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | No bleed air and power extraction for aircraft use. | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | Maximum allowable bleed air and power extraction for aircraft use. | Required |
| **(b)(3)** | An intermediate value for bleed air and power extraction representative of that which might be used as a maximum for aircraft during approach to a landing. | Required |
| **(c)** | If testing facilities are not available to demonstrate the effects of power extraction required in CS-E 745(b)(2) and (3), accomplish this through appropriate analytical means. | Required |

Sub-points (a) and (b) are different exercises on the same hardware. Sub-point
(a) is a pass or fail demonstration against named failure modes. Sub-point (b) is
a **measurement** of minimum response time, repeated at three load conditions.
It produces data rather than a verdict.

The load conditions in (a)(3) and (b) differ deliberately. The 5-second
demonstration uses "only the bleed air and accessories loads necessary to run the
Engine" [CS-E 745(a)(3)] — a clean engine. The (b) measurements sweep from no
aircraft bleed and offtake to the maximum allowable.

Sub-point (c) is a relief on facilities, not on evidence. Where power extraction
cannot be reproduced on the bed, analysis substitutes for test, but the result is
still required.

## Compliance

- Test bed demonstration of the one-second power demand increase to rated Take-off Power under the adverse bleed and power extraction combination [CS-E 745(a)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 745|AMC E 745(2)]].
- Demonstration of the 15 % to 95 % rated Take-off Power increase within 5 seconds from a stabilised static condition, or the justification for a longer time [CS-E 745(a)(3)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 745|AMC E 745(4)]].
- Minimum response time measurements at the three load conditions of (b) [CS-E 745(b)].
- Analytical substitute for the power extraction effects, where test facilities are unavailable [CS-E 745(c)].
- Definition of the minimum test bed idle condition used [CS-E 745(a)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 745|AMC E 745(3)]].
- Declaration of the maximum allowable bleed air and power extraction for aircraft use [CS-E 745(b)(2)], carried into the instructions for installation of [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]] and the assumptions of [[CS-E 30]].

## Application to this engine

The rotorcraft sub-points apply. Nothing here is restricted by rating.

**The acceleration is a control system demonstration.** A one-second power demand
step from minimum test bed idle to rated Take-off Power, without surge, stall or
over-temperature, is a direct test of the full-authority EECS scheduling. It
connects to the freedom from dangerous surge required by
[[CS-E 500|CS-E 500(a)]] and to the control system specifications of
[[CS-E 50]].

**The over-temperature threshold is defined elsewhere.**
[[AMC E 745|AMC E 745(2)]] states that an over-temperature event "is considered
as being any exceedence of the steady state and transient values which are
substantiated under CS-E 740". The acceleration test therefore cannot be
assessed until the endurance test of [[CS-E 740]] has fixed those values.
[[CS-E 740|CS-E 740(f)(4)(v)]] is where the rotorcraft transient EGT limit for
acceleration is established.

**The adverse combination differs by failure mode.**
[[AMC E 745|AMC E 745(2)]] distinguishes them. For over-temperature, the
adverse combination is probably maximum bleed air and maximum power extraction.
For surge and stall, it is probably no bleed air and maximum power extraction.
A single test condition does not cover both.

**Minimum test bed idle is defined for rotorcraft engines.**
[[AMC E 745|AMC E 745(3)]] gives it as "the minimum practically possible power
extraction from the Engine in the test facility while the output shaft is at the
governed speed". A governed output shaft speed is the free power-turbine
condition, which is why the rotorcraft sub-points use this idle rather than a
ground idle lever position.

The same idle condition and the same one-second step appear in
[[CS-E 740|CS-E 740(d)(1)(ii)]] for the endurance test accelerations, and in
[[CS-E 770|CS-E 770(c)]] for the low temperature acceleration.

## Not applicable

- **(a)(1)** — the acceleration demonstration for aeroplane engines, worded around moving a power or thrust control lever from the minimum flight idle position to the maximum position. The rotorcraft demonstration at (a)(2) applies instead.
- **(b)**, in part — the aeroplane wording covering movement of the power lever from minimum ground idle and minimum flight idle positions. The rotorcraft wording in the same sub-point applies.
