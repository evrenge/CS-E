# CS-E 170 — Engine Systems and Component Verification

> [!summary]
> The certification tests of CS-E exercise the engine as a whole, and some
> systems and components are not necessarily exercised by them. This paragraph
> closes that gap. Where a system or component cannot be adequately
> substantiated by the other tests of CS-E, additional tests or analyses must
> show it can perform its intended functions in all declared environmental and
> operating conditions.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | For those systems or components which cannot be adequately substantiated by other tests of CS-E, conduct additional tests or analyses to demonstrate that the systems or components are able to perform the intended functions in all declared environmental and operating conditions. | Required |

CS-E 170 is a single unnumbered sentence, so the `Ref` cell carries no sub-point
identifier.

Three parts of that sentence do the work. The trigger is inadequate
substantiation by the other tests of CS-E [CS-E 170]. The applicant identifies
where this is the case: the AMC asks the engine manufacturer to define all
necessary testing and analysis [AMC E 170]. The means is "additional tests
**or** analyses", so analysis is an accepted route and not a fallback. The
standard is "all declared environmental and operating conditions" [CS-E 170].
That is the full declared envelope, not the conditions the engine happened to
see on the test bed.

The scope is left open deliberately. [[AMC E 170]] gives examples rather than a
list, and states that the reasons for testing under CS-E 170 "include, but are
not limited to" those examples [AMC E 170].

## Compliance

- Identification of the systems and components not adequately substantiated by the other CS-E tests, with the reasoning for each [CS-E 170]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 170]].
- Additional test or analysis programme for each, demonstrating intended function across all declared environmental and operating conditions [CS-E 170]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 170]] and [[AMC E 80]].
- Declared environment for the Engine Control System and its components, entered into the engine instructions for installation as an installation limitation [CS-E 170]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 170]].
- Electrical bonding validity evidence, where HIRF or lightning testing is carried out on anything other than a representative complete engine [CS-E 170]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 170]], and see [[CS-E 135]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies in full; nothing in it is restricted by engine type or
rating.

CS-E 170 is one of the two paragraphs that [[AMC E 50|AMC E 50(2)]] names as
applying to control system components alongside [[CS-E 50]] itself. Individual
components such as alternators, sensors and actuators "should be covered, in
addition, under other CS-E paragraphs such as CS-E 80 or CS-E 170, as
appropriate". For a full-authority EECS, CS-E 170 is therefore a principal route
for substantiating the control system in its installed environment.
[[AMC E 170]] devotes most of its length to that case.

The endurance test that sets the baseline for "adequately substantiated" is
[[CS-E 740]]; [[AMC E 170]] also names CS-E 440, which is the piston endurance
test in Subpart C and does not apply here. Protective functions are the clearest
example for this engine. An over-speed protection system or a torque limiter is
unlikely to be exercised during the scheduled tests of CS-E 740, so it falls to
CS-E 170 [AMC E 170].
