# AMC E 70 — Castings, Forgings, Welded Structures and Welded Components

> [!summary]
> One AMC serves CS-E 70, and it covers three manufacturing families. Each
> family gets the same pattern: establish the quality controls, classify the part
> by the consequence of its failure, apply flaw or crack detection after heat
> treatment, put the manufacturing and quality-control information on the
> drawing, and freeze the supplier and the technique so neither changes without
> the engine constructor's agreement. Forgings carry two extra duties: integral
> test pieces, and residual stress assessment for Engine Critical Parts.

## Requirement

### AMC E 70(1) — castings

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(1)** | Establish the means of maintaining the required quality of all castings, by methods such as analysis for correct chemical composition, tests of mechanical properties, microscopic examination, break-up examination, strength tests and radiographic examination. | Accepted method |
| **(1)** | Carry out radiographic examination, where practicable, on the more highly stressed portions, to establish that the foundry technique is satisfactory. | Accepted method |
| **(1)** | Where radiographic examination is called for, continue it until a satisfactory standard of quality has been established. | Accepted method |
| **(1)** | Subsequent relaxation may be introduced in quantity production, at the engine constructor's discretion, using a system acceptable to the Agency. | Permitted |
| **(1)** | Subject all castings to a suitable flaw-detection process, completed after any heat treatment. | Accepted method |
| **(1)** | Put information sufficient to identify the means of manufacture and quality control on the drawings of each casting, by detail or by reference; identify areas of high stress where necessary, which may be done on a separate drawing. | Accepted method |
| **(1)** | Make no change of foundry or significant change of foundry technique without the agreement of the engine constructor, and include in that agreement a review of the need to repeat tests or revise the quality control method. | Accepted method |

### AMC E 70(2) — forgings

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(2)(a)** | Classify forgings as Class 1, Class 2 or Class 3: Class 1, parts whose Failure could hazard the aircraft; Class 2, stressed parts not covered by Class 1; Class 3, unstressed or only lightly stressed parts not covered by Class 1. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(a)** | Establish the means of maintaining the required quality of all forgings, by methods such as analysis for correct chemical composition, tests of mechanical properties, microscopic examination, fracture examination, strength tests and radiographic examination. | Accepted method |

| **(2)(a)** | Subject all forgings to a suitable crack-detection process at an appropriate stage, and repeat crack detection after any subsequent heat treatment. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(a)** | Where the level and location of residual stresses in forged Engine Critical Parts could be significant in relation to the intended loads, and cannot be assessed from experience on similar designs using similar materials and forging methods, carry out sufficient physical tests to give adequate assurance of the likely residual stress level and of freedom from unacceptable variability. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(a)** | Where radiographic or ultrasonic examination is called for, continue it until a satisfactory standard of quality has been established; relaxation may follow in quantity production at the engine constructor's discretion, using a system acceptable to the Agency. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(a)** | Put information sufficient to identify the means of manufacture and quality control on the drawings of each forging, by detail or by reference to process control documents. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(a)** | Prove the strength of Class 1 and Class 2 forgings satisfactory by calculation, by test, or by comparison with a forging of similar design already proved satisfactory. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(b)** | Normally incorporate one or more projections in each Class 1 and Class 2 forging which, after heat treatment, can serve as test pieces to establish that the material qualities are satisfactory. | Accepted method |

### AMC E 70(3) — welded structures and welded components

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(3)** | Classify fusion and resistance welds: Group 1, welds whose Failure or leakage could hazard the aircraft; Group 2, highly stressed welds whose Failure or leakage would not hazard the aircraft; Group 3, all other welds. | Accepted method |
| **(3)** | Establish the necessary means of maintaining the required quality of all welded structures and components, which may involve verifying correct application of the approved preparatory and welding techniques, destructive and non-destructive inspection of representative test specimens at prescribed intervals during weld production, visual inspection of each weld produced, and pressure testing of welds where applicable. | Accepted method |
| **(3)** | Subject all welds to a suitable crack-detection process at an appropriate stage, and repeat crack detection after any subsequent heat treatment. | Accepted method |
| **(3)** | Where radiographic examination is called for, continue it until a satisfactory standard of quality has been established; relaxation may follow in quantity production at the engine constructor's discretion, using a system acceptable to the Agency. | Accepted method |
| **(3)** | Put information sufficient to identify the means of welding and the quality control method on the drawings of each welded structure or component, by detail or by reference. | Accepted method |
| **(3)** | Make no significant change of welding technique without the agreement of the engine constructor, and include in that agreement a review of the need to revise the quality control method or to take modification approval action. | Accepted method |

The two classification schemes use different words for the same threshold. A
Class 1 forging is one whose "Failure … could hazard the aircraft" and a Group 1
weld is one whose "Failure or leakage of which could hazard the aircraft"
[AMC E 70(2)(a)], [AMC E 70(3)]. Welds add leakage as a failure mode; forgings do
not. Neither scheme uses the Hazardous Engine Effect classification of
[[CS-E 510]], so the two assessments are related but not interchangeable.

Crack and flaw detection are specified **after** heat treatment throughout. Heat
treatment can introduce or open defects, so inspection before it is not
sufficient evidence.

## Compliance

- Casting quality control plan naming the methods used, the highly stressed portions subject to radiography, and the flaw-detection process applied after heat treatment [AMC E 70(1)].
- Forging classification list assigning Class 1, Class 2 or Class 3 to each forged part [AMC E 70(2)(a)].
- Grain direction and agreed material properties shown on Class 1 forging drawings [AMC E 70(2)(a)].
- Residual stress assessment for forged Engine Critical Parts, by experience on similar designs or by physical test where experience is not available [AMC E 70(2)(a)]. This is the evidence that [[CS-E 100|CS-E 100(a)]] requires for residual stresses, and it belongs with the Engine Critical Part work of [[CS-E 515]].
- Strength proof for Class 1 and Class 2 forgings, by calculation, test or comparison [AMC E 70(2)(a)].
- Test-piece scheme for Class 1 and Class 2 forgings, with the forging manufacturer's certification, or a drawing statement and an agreed sample-testing technique where test pieces are unpractical [AMC E 70(2)(b)].
- Weld classification list assigning Group 1, Group 2 or Group 3 to each weld [AMC E 70(3)].
- Weld quality control plan covering technique verification, specimen inspection, visual inspection and pressure testing where applicable [AMC E 70(3)].
- Supplier and technique change control for foundries, forging constructors and welding techniques, with the review it triggers [AMC E 70(1)], [AMC E 70(2)(c)], [AMC E 70(3)].
- Manufacturing and quality-control information on the drawings for all three families [AMC E 70(1)], [AMC E 70(2)(a)], [AMC E 70(3)], within the drawing duties of [[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(a)]].

## Application to this engine

The whole AMC applies. Nothing in it is restricted by engine type, rating or
control system; it addresses manufacturing families, and a turboshaft contains
castings, forgings and welds.

The residual stress clause in (2)(a) is the one that connects directly to a
turbine-specific specification. [[CS-E 100|CS-E 100(a)]] requires due
consideration of residual stresses in Engine Critical Parts for turbine engines,
and AMC E 70(2)(a) gives the accepted means: experience on similar designs and
materials where it exists, physical tests where it does not. The parts in
question are identified under [[CS-E 515]].

The change-control clauses are continuing obligations rather than certification
milestones. A foundry change after certification triggers the same review, so the
control must survive into production and be reflected in the arrangements under
[[CS-E 25]].
