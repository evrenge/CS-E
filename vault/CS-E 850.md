# CS-E 850 — Compressor, Fan and Turbine Shafts

> [!summary]
> Shaft system Failures must not result in Hazardous Engine Effects, and the
> shaft systems must be designed so that Failures are predicted at a rate not
> exceeding Remote. Where the fail-safe objective cannot be met for certain
> elements of a shaft, those elements must instead be shown to fail at a rate not
> exceeding Extremely Remote — and that route is available only against five
> cumulative conditions, the first of which is that the shaft is an Engine
> Critical Part complying with CS-E 515.

## Requirement

### Objectives

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | Demonstrate that Failures of the shaft systems will not result in Hazardous Engine Effects, except as provided in CS-E 850(a)(3). | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Establish that the shaft systems are designed so that Failures are predicted to occur at a rate not in excess of that defined as Remote. | Required |
| **(a)(3)** | If compliance with the objective of CS-E 850(a)(1) is not achieved for certain elements of a shaft, show that Failures of these elements are predicted to occur at a rate not in excess of that defined as Extremely Remote. | Required |

The three objectives are not alternatives. Sub-point (a)(2) applies to the shaft
systems regardless: Failures at no worse than Remote, which is the
[[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(4)]] figure of less than 10⁻⁵ per engine flight hour.
Sub-point (a)(1) is the fail-safe objective, and (a)(3) is the fallback where it
cannot be met — a stricter probability, Extremely Remote, for those elements only.

[[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(3)]] is blunt about why (a)(3) exists: "In general,
experience has shown that Failures of shafts occur at a rate in excess of
Extremely Remote. Consequently, shaft systems should be designed to fail safe as
required by CS-E 850(a)(1)." It then adds that for conventional designs this is
not possible for all parts of a shaft system, "but the use of this provision
should be strictly limited."

### Compliance

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(b)(1)** | When it is claimed that Failures of the shaft systems will not result in Hazardous Engine Effects, a test will normally be required to demonstrate the consequences of these shaft Failures, unless it is agreed that the consequences are readily predictable. | Statement |
| **(b)(2)** | In complying with CS-E 850(a)(3), the Failure rate of certain elements of shaft systems will be accepted as Extremely Remote if (b)(2)(i) to (b)(2)(v) are all met. | Statement |
| **(b)(2)(i)** | The shaft is identified as an Engine Critical Part and compliance is shown with CS-E 515. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(2)(ii)** | Their material and design features are well understood and are conducive to well established and validated stressing techniques. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(2)(iii)** | The surrounding environment of the elements considered is such that a shaft Failure owing to this environment can be judged sufficiently unlikely that the Failure mode can be discounted. This consideration must include complexity of design, corrosion, wear, vibration, fire, contact with adjacent components or structure, overheating, and secondary effects from other Failures or combinations of Failures. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(2)(iv)** | In making the assessment described in CS-E 850(b)(2)(iii), any assumptions regarding the engine installation are identified and declared in accordance with CS-E 30. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(2)(v)** | Experience with parts of similar design is assessed and taken into account as appropriate. | Required if claimed |

The five conditions in (b)(2) are joined by "and" in the source and every one
must hold. Condition (i) is the heaviest: the shaft becomes an Engine Critical
Part, which brings the three plans of [[CS-E 515]] with it — Engineering,
Manufacturing and Service Management.

Condition (iii) lists eight environmental factors and the list is mandatory
rather than illustrative: the consideration "must include" all of them.

## Compliance

- Shaft system definition, identifying what transmits torque between the power-producing and power-using systems, including any engine gearbox [CS-E 850(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(1)(a)]].
- Demonstration that shaft Failures do not result in Hazardous Engine Effects — normally by test, unless the consequences are agreed readily predictable [CS-E 850(b)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(2)]].
- Probability assessment showing shaft system Failures no worse than Remote [CS-E 850(a)(2)], against [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(4)]].
- Where the fail-safe objective is not met for certain elements, evidence against all five conditions of (b)(2), including Engine Critical Part identification and [[CS-E 515]] compliance [CS-E 850(a)(3)].
- Installation assumptions declared under [[CS-E 30]] for the environmental assessment [CS-E 850(b)(2)(iv)].
- Design assessment covering undetected material defects, manufacturing tolerances, rubbing, bearing Failure effects, engine fire effects, shock loading and oscillatory loading [CS-E 850(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(4)(a)]].
- Shaft vibration survey evidence confirming whirling is not significant at any likely operating condition [CS-E 850(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(4)(b)(i)]], within [[CS-E 650]].
- Torsional fatigue evaluation of each shaft with an oscillatory torque of at least ± 5 % of the normal maximum steady-state torque superimposed [CS-E 850(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(4)(b)(ii)]].
- Inspectability assessment of the critical shaft section, where a Failure due to the environment is discounted [CS-E 850(b)(2)(iii)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(3)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies. A free power-turbine turboshaft has at least two shaft
systems: the gas generator shaft and the power turbine output shaft.
[[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(1)(a)]] includes "any Engine gearbox in that transmission
system" within the shaft definition.

**CS-E 850 is the escape route CS-E 840 depends on.**
[[CS-E 840|CS-E 840(c)]] requires the highest over-speed from a complete loss of
load on a turbine rotor to be included in the rotor integrity conditions "unless
it can be shown to be Extremely Remote under the provisions of CS-E 850". For a
free power turbine that is the central question of both paragraphs.

**Aircraft-side gearboxes are inside the analysis.**
[[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(2)(c)]] requires all likely Failure modes to be identified
"including the loss of loads caused by a Failure of any gearboxes supplied by the
aircraft manufacturer". For a rotorcraft that reaches the main transmission, so
the shaft Failure analysis crosses the engine boundary and its assumptions are
declared under [[CS-E 30]].

**A shaft failure caused by a blade failure is not excusable by probability.**
[[AMC E 810|AMC E 810(2)(c)(ii)]] gives this as the worked example: an effect
accepted as Extremely Remote under [[CS-E 810|CS-E 810(a)]] "would be
unacceptable under CS-E 850(a)(1) and/or CS-E 840(c)". Each paragraph must be
satisfied on its own terms.

**The service Failure modes are a checklist worth using.**
[[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(3)]] lists nine Failure modes that have led to shaft
Failures in service. Several of them connect directly to other paragraphs:
bearing degradation and bearing Failure to [[CS-E 570]], corrosion inside the
shaft to [[CS-E 90]], and fuel flow instability inducing shaft resonance to
[[CS-E 50]] and [[CS-E 650]]. An oil fire around the shaft connects to
[[CS-E 130]], and high cycle fatigue from a stress concentration feature
connects to [[CS-E 650|CS-E 650(f)]].

[VERIFY: which elements, if any, of the shaft systems rely on CS-E 850(a)(3)
rather than the fail-safe objective of CS-E 850(a)(1). Each such element becomes
an Engine Critical Part under CS-E 850(b)(2)(i), with the three plans of
CS-E 515, and AMC E 850(3) states that use of this provision should be strictly
limited.]
