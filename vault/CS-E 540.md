# CS-E 540 — Strike and Ingestion of Foreign Matter

> [!summary]
> This paragraph splits foreign matter by how many engines it is likely to
> affect. Matter likely to affect only one engine must not cause any Hazardous
> Engine Effect, with events below Extremely Remote excluded. Matter likely to
> affect more than one engine must not prevent continued safe flight and
> landing, whether through a Hazardous Engine Effect or through unacceptable
> loss of performance, deteriorated handling, or exceedance of an operating
> limitation.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Design the engine so that the strike and ingestion of foreign matter likely to affect only one engine in any one flight will not cause any Hazardous Engine Effects as defined in CS-E 510(g). | Required |
| **(a)** | Events with a probability of occurrence lower than Extremely Remote need not be considered. | Relief |
| **(b)** | Design the engine so that the strike and ingestion of foreign matter likely to affect more than one engine in any one flight will not preclude the continued safe flight and landing of the aircraft as a consequence of a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | The same applies to an unacceptable immediate or subsequent loss of performance. | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | The same applies to an unacceptable deterioration of engine handling characteristics. | Required |
| **(b)(3)** | The same applies to an unacceptable exceedance of any engine operating limitation. | Required |

The division is by exposure, not by object [CS-E 540]. The same object can fall
under (a) or (b) depending on whether an encounter is likely to reach one engine
or several.
[[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(2)]] gives the (b) population as "rain, hail, ice, gravel,
sand, small and medium birds".

The two sub-points set different standards. Sub-point (a) forbids a Hazardous
Engine Effect outright at engine level [CS-E 540(a)]. Sub-point (b) is an
aircraft-level test — continued safe flight and landing — and it adds three
named consequences beyond the Hazardous Engine Effect [CS-E 540(b)]. An
unacceptable loss of performance can fail (b) without any Hazardous Engine
Effect occurring.

The relief in (a) is the only probability cut-off in the paragraph, and it does
not extend to (b) [CS-E 540(a)].

## Compliance

- Identification of the foreign matter threats likely to affect one engine, and those likely to affect more than one [CS-E 540]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 540]].
- Demonstration that single-engine threats cause no Hazardous Engine Effect as defined in [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(g)]], excluding events below Extremely Remote [CS-E 540(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(1)]]; the large bird case is demonstrated under [[CS-E 800]].
- Substantiation of objects such as cleaning cloths, hand tools, rivets, bolts and screws, where their effects are likely to be more severe than the single large bird strike or ingestion [CS-E 540(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(1)]].
- Assessment of bird strike at speeds above 200 knots against the Extremely Remote criterion, with the corresponding probability of encountering a single bird [CS-E 540(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(1)]].
- Demonstration against continued safe flight and landing for multi-engine threats, covering performance loss, handling deterioration and operating limitation exceedance [CS-E 540(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(2)]]; quantified by [[CS-E 800]] for birds and [[CS-E 790]] for rain and hail.
- Assessment of any unusual finding from those demonstrations against the continued safe flight and landing objective [CS-E 540(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(2)]].

## Application to this engine

Both sub-points apply, and sub-point (b) carries particular weight for a
multi-engine rotorcraft. A threat that reaches more than one engine is assessed
against continued safe flight and landing of the aircraft. For this
installation, that is the condition the declared OEI ratings support.

CS-E 540 is a design specification rather than a test paragraph. The tests that
satisfy it are elsewhere: [[CS-E 800]] for bird strike and ingestion,
[[CS-E 790]] for rain and hail, [[CS-E 780]] for icing conditions, and the sand
and dust exposure in [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(a)]].
[[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(2)]] states that those related paragraphs "are therefore
intended to be sufficient for demonstrating compliance with CS-E 540(b) for the
considered subject", so CS-E 540 is not a second set of tests.

The 200-knot consideration in [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(1)]] is an exception to
that. It asks for the possibility of operation above 200 knots to be considered
in verifying the Extremely Remote criterion for bird strike. It is stated as an
addition to the CS-E 800 demonstration, rather than as part of it.

Foreign matter ingress into bleed-fed areas is handled separately by
[[CS-E 580]], and contaminated fuel by [[CS-E 670]].
