# CS-E 670 — Contaminated Fuel

> [!summary]
> The complete engine fuel system must be shown to function satisfactorily with
> the maximum quantity of liquid and solid contamination likely to be encountered
> in service, for long enough to ensure no engine malfunction results. The
> evidence must cover two things: that contamination passing through the
> filtration does no harm, immediately or later, and that the engine can keep
> running for at least half the maximum flight duration after impending filter
> blockage is first indicated.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Provide evidence that the complete engine fuel system is capable of functioning satisfactorily with fuel containing the maximum quantity of liquid and solid contamination likely to be encountered in service, for a period sufficient to ensure that engine malfunctioning as a result of this cause will not occur. | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | Provide assurance that the fuel system is not adversely affected by contamination which can pass through any filtration provided, either immediately or during subsequent running. | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | Provide assurance that it will be possible for the engine to complete a period equal to at least half the maximum flight duration of the aeroplane in which it is likely to be installed, with the same contaminant level, from the point at which indication of impending filter blockage is first given. | Required |

Sub-point (b)(1) concerns what gets **through** the filter, not what the filter
catches. Filtration therefore does not answer (b)(1): the question is whether the
system tolerates the particles fine enough to pass it, both at once and
cumulatively.

Sub-point (b)(2) starts its clock at the **indication** of impending blockage,
not at actual blockage. The indication is the one required by
[[CS-E 560|CS-E 560(b)(2)]], so the two paragraphs are linked: CS-E 560 requires
the warning, CS-E 670 fixes how long the engine must keep running after it.

"The same contaminant level" in (b)(2) means the continued running is at the
contamination that triggered the indication, not at clean-fuel conditions.

## Compliance

- Definition of the maximum quantity of liquid and solid contamination likely to be encountered in service [CS-E 670(a)]. Accepted means: the contaminant specification of [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(a)]].
- Complete fuel system test on a running engine or on a rig, with continuously contaminated fuel [CS-E 670(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(b)]], [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(d)]].
- Determination of the point at which impending filter blockage is indicated to the flight crew [CS-E 670(b)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(c)]]; the indication itself is required by [[CS-E 560|CS-E 560(b)(2)]].
- Demonstration of continued operation without engine malfunction for at least half the maximum flight duration after that indication [CS-E 670(b)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(c)]].
- Water contaminant test with fuel saturated at 27 °C plus 0.2 ml of free water per litre, cooled to the most critical icing condition [CS-E 670(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(2)]].
- Transient fuel icing operability consideration [CS-E 670(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(3)]] and [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(4)]].

## Application to this engine

Both sub-points apply. Neither is restricted by engine type or rating.

[VERIFY: CS-E 670(b)(2) is written as "half the maximum flight duration of the
aeroplane in which it is likely to be installed". [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(c)]]
states the same duty as "half the maximum flight duration of the aircraft in
which it is likely to be installed". The specification says aeroplane and the
accepted means says aircraft. The AMC wording appears to be the operative one for
a rotorcraft installation, but CS-E does not settle it. The value itself — the
maximum flight duration of the intended rotorcraft — is also not recorded in
`engine_profile.md`.]

The contaminant list in [[AMC E 670|AMC E 670(1)(a)]] includes a carbon fibre rod
specification that applies only "for engines to be fitted to Aircraft with Carbon
Fibre Composite Material Fuel Tanks". Whether it applies here depends on the
rotorcraft's fuel tank construction, which is an installation assumption under
[[CS-E 30]].

Sand and dust are prominent in the contaminant list, and a rotorcraft turboshaft
is exposed to them. The related duties are the bleed air protection of
[[CS-E 580]] and the sand and dust qualification of
[[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(a)]], both of which address ingress rather than fuel-borne
contamination.
