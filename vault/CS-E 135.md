# CS-E 135 — Electrical Bonding

> [!summary]
> Anything on the engine that can produce or suffer static discharge, or carry
> current from an electrical Fault, must be grounded to the main engine earth.
> The specification is written against three consequences rather than against a
> resistance figure: electric shock injury, unintentional ignition where
> flammable fluids or vapours could be present, and unacceptable interference
> with electrical or electronic equipment. Bonding must be sufficient to minimise
> the charge accumulation that would cause any of the three.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Design and construct any components, modules, equipment and accessories that are susceptible to, or are potential sources of, static discharges or currents from electrical Faults so as to be grounded to the main engine earth, as necessary to minimise the accumulation of electro-static or electrical charge. | Required |
| — | The charge accumulation to be minimised is that which would cause injury from electrical shock. | Required |
| — | …or unintentional ignition in areas where flammable fluids or vapours could be present. | Required |
| — | …or unacceptable interference with electrical or electronic equipment. | Required |

CS-E 135 is a single unnumbered paragraph with a three-item list, so the `Ref`
cells carry no sub-point identifier.

The test is consequence-based. The specification names no bonding resistance and
no test method; it requires grounding "as necessary to minimise" the accumulation
that would cause one of the three listed effects [CS-E 135]. The applicant
therefore has to establish which components fall in scope and what "as necessary"
means for each, and [[AMC E 135]] gives the accepted means.

Scope is set by two properties, either of which is sufficient: a component may be
**susceptible to** static discharge or Fault current, or a **potential source** of
it.

## Compliance

- Identification of the components, modules, equipment and accessories that are susceptible to, or sources of, static discharge or Fault current [CS-E 135].
- Evidence that each is bonded to the main engine earth [CS-E 135]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 135]] — examination of the type design drawings, electrical continuity checks, or inspection of a representative engine.
- Evidence that a main engine earth exists [CS-E 135]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 135]].
- Assessment against the three consequences: electrical shock, ignition in flammable fluid or vapour areas, and interference with electrical or electronic equipment [CS-E 135].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies in full; nothing in it is restricted by engine type or
rating.

The three consequences route to three different parts of the compliance
programme. Ignition in areas where flammable fluids or vapours could be present
connects to the fire protection work of [[CS-E 130]]. It also connects to the
explosion proofness item in [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(b)]], which distinguishes the
fuel system environment from the Flammable Fluid Leakage area. Interference with
electrical or electronic equipment connects to the EMI, HIRF and lightning item
in [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(b)]]. For a full-authority EECS, it also connects to
the control system integrity specifications of [[CS-E 50]].

For this engine the interference consequence carries the most weight. An EECS
concentrates electronic equipment on the engine. Therefore, the bonding argument
is part of the evidence that the control system is not disturbed by static or
Fault currents originating elsewhere on the engine.
