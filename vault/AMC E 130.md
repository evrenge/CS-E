# AMC E 130 — Fire Protection

> [!summary]
> One long AMC serves CS-E 130. It defines the terms the specification leaves
> open, including a working figure for a hazardous quantity, then explains how
> to decide whether a component needs the Fire-resistant or the Fireproof level.
> It sets the pass and fail criteria for a fire test, addresses the fire
> behaviour of titanium, magnesium and abradable linings, and gives detailed
> conditions for the flammable fluid tank test. Shorter sections cover drains
> and vents, bleed air, firewalls and shielding.

## Requirement

### AMC E 130(1) — definitions

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(1)(a)** | Drain and Vent Systems are components used to convey unused or unwanted quantities of flammable fluid or vapour away from the engine. | Statement |
| **(1)(b)** | External Lines, Fittings and Other Components are engine parts conveying flammable fluids that are external to the main engine casings, frames and other major structure, including but not limited to fuel or oil tubes, accessory gearbox, pumps, heat exchangers, valves and engine fuel control units. | Statement |
| **(1)(c)** | A Fire Hazard is the unintentional release or collection of a hazardous quantity of flammable fluid, vapour or other materials; or a Failure or malfunction resulting in an unintentional ignition source within a fire zone; or the potential for a Hazardous Engine Effect as a result of exposure to a fire. | Statement |
| **(1)(d)** | The definitions of "Fire-resistant" and "Fireproof" are given in CS-Definitions; they imply that the functioning of the part under fire condition should not hazard the aircraft. | Statement |
| **(1)(e)** | A hazardous quantity is an amount of fluid, vapour or other material which could sustain a fire of sufficient time and severity to create damage potentially leading to a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(1)(e)** | In the absence of a more suitable determination, a hazardous quantity can be assumed to be 0.25 litre or more of fuel, or a quantity of flammable material of equivalent heat content. | Permitted |

[VERIFY: unlisted construction. Row (1)(e) uses "can be assumed" and carries

The 0.25 litre figure is a default, not a threshold fixed by the rule. It
applies "In the absence of a more suitable determination of a hazardous quantity
of flammable fluid" [AMC E 130(1)(e)], so a substantiated engine-specific figure
displaces it.

### AMC E 130(2) — intent, level of protection and pass/fail criteria

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(2)(a)** | The intent of CS-E 130 is to give assurance that the design, materials and construction techniques used will minimise the probability of the occurrence, the consequences and the spread of fire. | Statement |
| **(2)(b)** | The primary objectives are to contain, isolate and withstand a fire or prevent sources of flammable material or air from feeding an existing fire; and to increase the probability that the Engine Control System and accessories will permit a safe shutdown of the engine and subsequently maintain that condition. | Statement |
| **(2)(c)** | Determine which level of fire protection should be shown for each component requiring a fire protection evaluation. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(c)** | In general, components conveying flammable fluids can be evaluated to a Fire-resistant standard provided the normal supply of flammable fluid is stopped by a shutoff feature. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(c)** | Oil system components may need to be evaluated from the standpoint of fire hazard — quantity, pressure, flow rate and similar — to determine whether the Fire-resistant or the Fireproof standard should apply. Historically, most oil system components have been evaluated to a Fireproof standard. | Statement |
| **(2)(c)** | Evaluate other flammable fluid conveying components, except flammable fluid tanks, in a similar manner. Flammable fluid tanks should be Fireproof as required by CS-E 130(c). | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)** | When a fire test is performed, consider these acceptance criteria: the ability to perform the functions intended in case of fire is maintained; no leakage of hazardous quantities of flammable fluids, vapours or other materials; no support of combustion by the constituent material of the article tested; no burn through of firewalls; no other conditions which could produce Hazardous Engine Effects. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(i)** | The functions intended to be provided in case of fire are determined case by case. For example, an Engine Control System should not cause a Hazardous Engine Effect while continuing to operate, but should allow or may cause a safe shutdown of the engine at any time within the required exposure time period. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(i)** | A safe engine shutdown at any time during the fire resistance test is an acceptable outcome for such a component, provided the safe shutdown is maintained until the end of the 5 minutes test period. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(i)** | A flammable fluid tank shutoff valve should be operable to close, or should default closed, and should maintain that position without leakage of a hazardous quantity of flammable fluid until the end of the 15 minute test period. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(ii)** | At no time during or at the end of the test should the test article leak a hazardous quantity of flammable fluid. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(iii)** | Treat a non-self-extinguishing fire test event as cause for Failure of the test, unless it can be shown that the constituent material supporting combustion is not a hazardous quantity of flammable fluid, vapour or material as defined in this AMC. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(iv)** | At no time during or at the end of the test should a firewall component fail to contain the fire within the intended zone or area: no burn through hole, no failure at attachment or fire seal points around the periphery, no continued burning after the test flame is removed, and no backside ignition. | Accepted method |
| **(2)(d)(v)** | At no time during or at the end of the test should a Hazardous Engine Effect result. | Accepted method |

The exposure times are stated here rather than in the specification.
Fire-resistant carries a 5-minute exposure, which AMC E 130(2)(c) justifies as
"a reasonable time period for the flight crew to recognise a fire condition,
shut down the appropriate Engine and close the appropriate fuel shutoff
valve(s)". The Fireproof tank shutoff valve case runs to 15 minutes
[AMC E 130(2)(d)(i)].

The electronics exception in (2)(d)(iii) is narrow. Circuit board potting
compounds inside Engine Control System casings may support a small intensity
fire after the test flame is removed [AMC E 130(2)(d)(iii)]. A
non-self-extinguishing event still fails the test, unless it can be shown that
the material supporting combustion is not a hazardous quantity
[AMC E 130(2)(d)(iii)].

### AMC E 130(3) — materials

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(3)(a)** | Experience has shown that when using materials such as magnesium and titanium alloys, appropriate design precautions may be required to prevent an unacceptable fire hazard. | Statement |
| **(3)(a)** | Consideration should be given to the possibility of fire as a result of rubbing or contact with hot gases. | Accepted method |
| **(3)(a)** | Any material used for abradable linings needs to be assessed to ensure that fire or explosion hazards are avoided. | Required |
| **(3)(a)** | Give consideration also to the effects of mechanical Failure of any engine component and to dimensional changes from thermal effects within the engine. | Accepted method |
| **(3)(b)** | In showing compliance with CS-E 130(a), assess the overall design for vulnerability to titanium fires. If the assessment cannot rule out the possibility of a sustained fire, show that a titanium fire does not result in a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Accepted method |
| **(3)(c)** | In showing compliance with CS-E 130, assess the overall design for vulnerability to magnesium fires. If the assessment cannot rule out a sustained fire, show that a magnesium fire would be confined to areas within the engine such that it does not result in a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Accepted method |
| **(3)(c)** | Carefully evaluate the use of magnesium alloys in thin sections, or where they are exposed to corrosion, rubbing or high scrubbing speeds. | Accepted method |
| **(3)(d)** | Evaluate fire and explosion risk from abradable linings for each fan, compressor and turbine stage that has an abradable lining. | Accepted method |
| **(3)(e)** | Absorbent materials should not be used in close proximity to flammable fluid system components unless they are treated or covered to prevent the absorption of a hazardous quantity of such fluid. | Accepted method |

[VERIFY: unlisted construction. Row (3)(a) uses "needs to" and carries Required.
The phrasing is neither one of the seven source verbs nor a listed declaratory

The titanium precautions listed in (3)(b) are described as measures that "can
reduce the susceptibility of Engines to titanium fires" rather than as
requirements. They include alloy constituents, and blade or casing coatings or
mechanical linings that inhibit ignition or combustion. They also include design
measures that minimise dangerous rubs: large inter-blade-row clearances,
abradable materials of sufficient depth in rub areas, and avoiding titanium for
adjacent rotating and static parts. The measures further include accounting for
rotor movements under transient and bearing Failure conditions, and ensuring
that thin, easily ignited titanium sections are unlikely to be shed at the front
of the engine [AMC E 130(3)(b)].

### AMC E 130(5) — flammable fluid tank fire test

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(5)** | In the absence of an acceptable installation assessment, apply the fire test flame to the tank locations or features determined by analysis or test to be most critical with respect to fire susceptibility. | Accepted method |
| **(5)** | Consider the tank installation and all features of the tank assembly when selecting the flame application location, including tank body, inlet and outlet assemblies, sight glass, drain plug, magnetic chip detector, quantity sender assembly, vent line assembly, filler cap and scupper, mounts, shutoff valve, temperature sensor and air/fluid separator assembly. | Accepted method |
| **(5)** | In some instances, compliance with CS-E 130 may need to be supported by data from other fire tests, multiple location testing, sub-component level tests, or service experience, to cover all tank assembly features. | Statement |
| **(5)** | Consider vent system performance, the lack of heat sink effect for tank features at or above the operating fluid level, and the effect of any special protective features. | Accepted method |
| **(5)** | Set the tank quantity at the start of the test no greater than the minimum dispatchable quantity, unless a greater quantity is more severe. | Accepted method |
| **(5)** | Conduct the first 5 minutes of the test at the most critical operating condition, typically a minimum flight idle flow rate, and the subsequent 10 minutes at an engine shutdown flow rate with consideration of the effect of any continued rotation. | Accepted method |
| **(5)** | The test may be run, at the applicant's option, for 15 minutes at the most critical condition — the worst case of engine operating or in-flight shutdown conditions. | Permitted |
| **(5)** | Set the fluid temperature at its maximum value, the greatest of the steady state or transient limit, at the start of the test, unless a lower temperature is more severe; and the tank internal pressure at the normal working pressure for the operating conditions at the start of the test. | Accepted method |
| **(5)** | Review the tank design and its intended application to provide reasonable assurance that the test set-up reflects the most critical flame impingement orientation and operating conditions. | Accepted method |

The tank test is the one place where the AMC gives an explicit time structure:
5 minutes at the critical operating condition, then 10 minutes at shutdown flow,
or 15 minutes at the worst case throughout. The AMC notes from experience that
"oil tank fire tests have failed due to high internal pressure and inadequate
venting" [AMC E 130(5)].

## Compliance

- Component classification list assigning the Fire-resistant or Fireproof level to each component requiring a fire protection evaluation, with the shutoff feature relied on where Fire-resistant is claimed [AMC E 130(2)(c)].
- Hazardous quantity determination, or a record that the 0.25 litre default is used [AMC E 130(1)(e)].
- Fire test reports against the five pass/fail criteria, with the functions intended in case of fire defined for each test article [AMC E 130(2)(d)].
- Burner and flame calibration records to ISO 2685, pre-test for every test, plus constancy evidence or a post-test calibration [AMC E 130(4)(a)].
- Flame impingement location justification, by the critical-feature route or the installation analysis route; where the latter is used, the installer co-ordination record and the re-evaluation process for each new installation [AMC E 130(4)(b)].
- Test article operating parameter justification showing conservatism [AMC E 130(4)(c)].
- Titanium and magnesium fire vulnerability assessments, with the Hazardous Engine Effect demonstration where a sustained fire cannot be ruled out [AMC E 130(3)(b)], [AMC E 130(3)(c)].
- Abradable lining fire and explosion evaluation for each fan, compressor and turbine stage having one [AMC E 130(3)(d)].
- Absorbent material survey near flammable fluid system components [AMC E 130(3)(e)].
- Flammable fluid tank fire test with its quantity, flow rate, temperature and pressure conditions recorded [AMC E 130(5)].
- Drain and vent function review, identifying any that would flow a hazardous quantity during continued rotation [AMC E 130(6)].
- Bleed air component fire evaluation [AMC E 130(7)].
- Firewall substantiation against passage around the firewall and Hazardous Engine Effects [AMC E 130(8)].
- Shielding and location substantiation, with the ignition sources identified [AMC E 130(9)].
- Electrical system component fire evaluation, showing cables, connectors, terminals and equipment in designated fire zones are at least Fire-resistant [AMC E 130(4)(d)].

## Application to this engine

The AMC applies throughout. Four parts of it bear directly on this
configuration.

**Continued rotation and the oil system.** AMC E 130(2)(c) states
that oil system components of turbine engines may keep flowing oil after
shutdown because of continued rotation, and that the supply to a fire "might
exist for as long as the continued rotation effects are present or until the oil
supply is depleted". The shutoff argument that supports a Fire-resistant rating
for fuel components therefore does not carry across to oil components
automatically [AMC E 130(2)(c)]. Paragraph (6) applies the same reasoning to
drains and vents [AMC E 130(6)]. This connects to [[CS-E 525]] and
[[CS-E 570|CS-E 570(e)(1)]].

**The Engine Control System.** Paragraph (2)(d)(i) sets the acceptable test
outcome for a control system component, and (4)(d) asks for the electrical
system evaluation. With a full-authority EECS, both reach a large population of
components. The potting compound case in (2)(d)(iii) describes exactly this
kind of hardware. The fire item in [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(c)]] points here.

**The two fire levels.** Point (1)(d) says only that the definitions are in
CS-Definitions and what they imply. The definitions themselves are recorded in
[[CS-E 130]]: one flame, 15 minutes for Fireproof and 5 minutes for
Fire-resistant. The exposure times in this AMC are the same two numbers, so a
component classified here is being classified against those definitions.

**Installation dependence.** The installation analysis route in (4)(b) calls
for co-ordination with the installer and re-evaluation for each new installation
[AMC E 130(4)(b)]. Since the rotorcraft installation is not fixed, choosing that
route now would create a recurring obligation for every future installation.

## Not applicable

- **(2)(b)**, in part — the objective clause "or feathering of the Propeller (if the Propeller control system is part of the Engine design)". A turboshaft driving a rotorcraft transmission has no propeller, and propeller material is excluded from this vault by scope. The safe engine shutdown half of the same objective applies.
- **(2)(c)**, in part — thrust augmentation systems, given as an example of other flammable fluid conveying components. Thrust augmentation does not arise on this engine; the hydraulic system example in the same sentence does apply.

## Amendment history

Amended at Amendment 8. The change is a single cross-reference correction in
paragraph (4)(d), Electrical Systems components:

- **Before:** "For compliance with CS-E 130(c), the effects of fire on components of the electrical system should be evaluated."
- **After:** "For compliance with CS-E 130(e), the effects of fire on components of the electrical system should be evaluated."

Amendment 8 deleted "c" and inserted "e". The correction matters for reading,
even though no wording of the guidance itself changed. CS-E 130(c) is the
flammable fluid tank specification; CS-E 130(e) is the specification that engine
control system components in a designated fire zone must be at least
Fire-resistant. The electrical system evaluation now points at the sub-point it
actually supports. The obligation on the applicant is unchanged.

The paragraph carries `[Amdt No: E/1]`, `[Amdt No: E/5]` and `[Amdt No: E/8]`.
