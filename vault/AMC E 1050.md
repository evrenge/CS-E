# AMC E 1050 — Exposure to volcanic cloud hazards

> [!summary]
> This AMC gives the means for establishing susceptibility and the content of the
> information operators need. Susceptibility is established by a combination of
> experience, studies, analysis, and/or testing. A volcanic cloud is more than
> ash, and the other constituents should be assessed too. Four points structure the
> work: identify the susceptible features, state the nature and severity of the
> effects, define the operator precautions, and recommend the continued
> airworthiness inspections.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Establish the susceptibility of engine features to the effects of volcanic clouds by a combination of experience, studies, analysis, and/or testing of parts, sub-assemblies or engines. | Accepted method |
| — | Contain the information necessary for safe operation in the relevant documentation. | Accepted method |
| — | That information may be used to assist operators in producing operational data and instructions for their flight crews when operating in, or avoiding, airspace contaminated with volcanic clouds. | Permitted |
| — | Make the information readily usable by operators in preparing a safety risk assessment as part of their overall management system. | Accepted method |
| — | A volcanic cloud comprises volcanic ash together with gases and other chemicals. The primary hazard is volcanic ash, and other elements of the volcanic cloud may also be undesirable to operate through. | Statement |
| — | Assess the effect on airworthiness of the other elements of the volcanic cloud. | Accepted method |
| **(1)** | Identify the features of the turbine engine that are susceptible to airworthiness effects from volcanic clouds. | Accepted method |
| **(1)(a)** | Those features may include erosion of compressor blades and other internal parts. | Statement |
| **(1)(b)** | …glassy deposits on hot section parts, which can result in loss of surge margins, engine stall, flame out, and inability to restart engines. | Statement |
| **(1)(c)** | …clogging of turbine blade cooling channels. | Statement |
| **(1)(d)** | …corrosion of metallic parts. | Statement |
| **(1)(e)** | …oil and fuel circuit contamination. | Statement |
| **(1)(f)** | …electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic systems. | Statement |
| **(2)** | Consider the nature and severity of effects. | Accepted method |
| **(3)** | Consider the related pre-flight, in-flight and post-flight precautions to be observed by the operator, including any necessary amendments to engine manuals, Dispatch Deviation, or equivalents, required to support the operator. | Accepted method |
| **(4)** | Consider the recommended continued airworthiness inspections associated with operations in volcanic cloud contaminated airspace; these may take the form of Instructions for Continued Airworthiness or other advice. | Accepted method |

The AMC opens with unnumbered prose before the four numbered points, so those
`Ref` cells carry no sub-point identifier.

The sentence on the composition of a volcanic cloud carries two verbs and is
split into two rows. "other elements of the volcanic cloud may also be
undesirable to operate through" states what those elements might do. It grants
the applicant nothing. So it is labelled Statement rather than Permitted. The
duty in the same sentence is the one that follows: "their effect on airworthiness
should be assessed" [AMC E 1050].

The feature list in (1) is explicitly open: the features "may include but are not
limited to the following" [AMC E 1050(1)].

The information is not only for the applicant's own use. It exists so operators
can produce operational data and instructions for their flight crews, and can
prepare a safety risk assessment as part of their management system
[AMC E 1050].

Point (3) reaches documents outside the engine manuals. The amendments it names
are to "Engine Manuals, Dispatch Deviation, or equivalents" [AMC E 1050(3)],
which makes the output span the operator's document set rather than only the
type certificate holder's.

## Compliance

- Susceptibility assessment combining experience, studies, analysis, and/or testing of parts, sub-assemblies or engines [AMC E 1050].
- Assessment of the non-ash constituents of a volcanic cloud — gases and other chemicals — for their effect on airworthiness [AMC E 1050].
- Identified list of susceptible engine features, covering at least erosion, glassy deposits, cooling channel clogging, corrosion, oil and fuel circuit contamination, and electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic systems [AMC E 1050(1)].
- Statement of the nature and severity of each effect [AMC E 1050(2)].
- Pre-flight, in-flight and post-flight operator precautions, with the amendments they require to the engine manuals and Dispatch Deviation, or equivalents [AMC E 1050(3)].
- Recommended continued airworthiness inspections for operation in contaminated airspace, as Instructions for Continued Airworthiness or other advice [AMC E 1050(4)], within [[CS-E 25]].
- Documentation in a form readily usable by operators preparing a safety risk assessment [AMC E 1050].

## Application to this engine

The AMC applies if the applicant elects compliance with [[CS-E 1050]], which is
optional under [[CS-E 1000]].

Each of the six named feature categories connects to work required elsewhere, so
electing this paragraph largely reuses existing evidence rather than generating
new tests:

- **(1)(a)** erosion of compressor blades and internal parts — the foreign matter design duty of [[CS-E 540]] and the bleed air protection of [[CS-E 580]], with the sand and dust qualification in [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(a)]].
- **(1)(b)** glassy deposits causing loss of surge margin, stall, flame out and inability to restart — freedom from dangerous surge and instability under [[CS-E 500|CS-E 500(a)]], and the relighting in flight tests of [[CS-E 910]].
- **(1)(c)** clogging of turbine blade cooling channels — the turbine cooling system monitoring of [[CS-E 60|CS-E 60(e)]], and the bleed air foreign matter duty of [[CS-E 580]].
- **(1)(d)** corrosion of metallic parts — [[CS-E 90]], and the salt spray and fluid susceptibility items of [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(a)]].
- **(1)(e)** oil and fuel circuit contamination — the filtration and by-pass provisions of [[CS-E 560]] and [[CS-E 570]], and the contaminated fuel test of [[CS-E 670]].
- **(1)(f)** electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic systems — [[CS-E 80]] and, for a full-authority EECS, [[CS-E 50]].

Point (1)(b) is the one that reaches an engine-level argument rather than a
component one. A volcanic cloud is encountered by every engine of the aircraft
at once. So an inability to restart is not an independent per-engine event.

[VERIFY: whether that common exposure is assessed under CS-E 540(b). That
sub-point is the multi-engine limb: it covers foreign matter "that is likely to
affect more than one Engine in any one flight" and requires that it not preclude
the continued safe flight and landing of the aircraft [CS-E 540(b)]. A volcanic
cloud matches the limb, but AMC E 1050 does not make the link, and AMC E 540(2)
lists what CS-E 540(b) is aimed at — "rain, hail, ice, gravel, sand, small and
medium birds" — without naming volcanic cloud.]
