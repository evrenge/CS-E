# CS-E 860 — Turbine Rotor Over-temperature

> [!summary]
> The applicant must establish the most critical temperature conditions the
> turbine rotors can reach when the cooling air supply fails. Analysis and tests
> are used as appropriate, and component Failures classified as Extremely Remote
> may be left out. The paragraph also states how evidence is obtained when the
> applicant wants to show that turbine cooling instrumentation is not required
> under CS-E 60(e).

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Establish, by analysis and tests as appropriate, the most critical temperature conditions the turbine rotor(s) can attain in the event of Failures of the cooling air supply. | Required |
| **(a)** | Failure of individual components of the engine that can be classified as Extremely Remote need not be included in the analysis or tests. | Relief |
| **(b)** | Evidence to demonstrate that instrumentation is not required under CS-E 60(e) may be obtained from endurance running in an engine or on rigs, or, where adequate margins can be demonstrated, by calculation. | Permitted |
| **(b)** | Where practicable, the duration of endurance running may be reduced by compensating increases in the test temperature. | Permitted |

Sub-point (a) is a substantiation obligation, not a limit. It does not set a
maximum turbine rotor temperature; it requires the worst case reachable after a
cooling air Failure to be known. What is then done with that temperature belongs
to the rotor integrity and life substantiation.

The relief in (a) is narrow. It removes only individual component Failures that
can be classified as Extremely Remote [CS-E 860(a)]. [[CS-E 15]] defines that
term as unlikely to occur in the total operational life of a number of aircraft
of the type, but still possible [CS-E 15(b)]. The relief does not remove the
cooling air supply Failure itself, which is the subject of the paragraph.

Sub-point (b) is written as a permission and connects this paragraph to
[[CS-E 60|CS-E 60(e)]]. That paragraph requires instrumentation enabling the
flight crew to monitor the functioning of the turbine cooling system, unless
evidence shows one of three conditions. CS-E 860(b) states the acceptable
sources of that evidence: endurance running in an engine, endurance running on
rigs, or calculation where adequate margins can be demonstrated. The
time-temperature trade is explicit: endurance running may be shortened by
raising the test temperature, where practicable.

## Compliance

- Analysis of the cooling air supply Failure modes and the resulting turbine rotor temperatures, identifying the most critical condition [CS-E 860(a)].
- Tests where analysis alone is not adequate [CS-E 860(a)]. The source leaves the split between analysis and test to "as appropriate".
- Probability substantiation for any individual component Failure excluded as Extremely Remote [CS-E 860(a)], against [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(3)]].
- Endurance running evidence, in an engine or on rigs, or a margin calculation, where instrumentation is claimed not to be required [CS-E 860(b)] for [[CS-E 60|CS-E 60(e)]].
- Justification of any compensating temperature increase used to shorten endurance running [CS-E 860(b)].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies. For a turboshaft with cooled turbine rotors, the
applicant must establish the most critical rotor temperatures after a cooling
air Failure [CS-E 860(a)]. The search for the most critical conditions therefore
covers the declared OEI ratings, not only a nominal operating condition.

**The instrumentation decision is made in CS-E 60, and evidenced here.**
[[CS-E 60|CS-E 60(e)]] states three exceptions to the duty to provide turbine
cooling instrumentation — other instrumentation gives adequate warning, the
Failure does not lead to Hazardous Engine Effects before detection, or the
Failure probability is Extremely Remote. CS-E 860(b) states what evidence
supports that claim. The two paragraphs are worked together, and the outcome
feeds the list of instruments in the Engine instructions for installation
[CS-E 60(b)].

**The established over-temperature condition feeds the life and integrity case.**
The temperatures found under CS-E 860(a) are an input to two things. They feed
the turbine rotor integrity demonstration in [[CS-E 840]], which requires the
material temperatures and temperature gradients to be "the most adverse which
could be achieved" [AMC E 840(4)(a)]. They also feed the Engine Critical Part
life substantiation under [[CS-E 515]].

**This is not the exhaust gas over-temperature test.**
[[CS-E 870]] is a separate, elective test that establishes a Maximum Exhaust Gas
Over-temperature limit. CS-E 860 is mandatory and concerns the turbine rotor
after a cooling air Failure. The two paragraphs are adjacent in the source but
carry different obligations.

[VERIFY: whether turbine cooling system instrumentation is provided, or whether
one of the three CS-E 60(e) exceptions is claimed. The choice determines whether
the endurance running or calculation evidence of CS-E 860(b) is required.]
