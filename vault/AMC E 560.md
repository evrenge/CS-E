# AMC E 560 — Fuel System

> [!summary]
> Nine points serve CS-E 560. More than one fuel type may be approved, and the
> specification applies to each. Contaminants include those generated by wear
> inside the engine fuel system, not only those delivered by the aircraft. The
> longest point covers transient fuel icing: where the aircraft manufacturer has
> not completed a threat assessment, the applicant assesses it or declares that
> no capability has been demonstrated. Three further points cover assembly error,
> filter mounting and servicing, and protection of control settings.

Point (4) defines its own term: "A transient fuel icing condition is considered
to be a short-duration exposure to high concentrations of (water) ice in the fuel
delivered to the Engine that is caused by the accumulation and subsequent
shedding of ice within the aircraft fuel system" [AMC E 560(4)]. The ice
originates in the **aircraft** fuel system, not the engine's, which is why the
threat assessment belongs to the aircraft manufacturer in the first instance.

The fallback in (4) is explicit and is a genuine alternative: assess the potential
threat, **or** declare that no capability has been demonstrated. Declaring no
capability is an accepted outcome, but it becomes an installation limitation
under [[CS-E 30|CS-E 30(a)]] and [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].

Point (2) widens the contaminant population beyond what the aircraft delivers.
Wear debris from the engine's own fuel system — a fuel pump bearing is the
example — counts as a contaminant the filtration must handle.

Points (5) and (6) serve specifications outside CS-E 560. Point (5) is the fuel
system instance of [[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(d)]] on incorrect assembly, and point (6)
is the fuel system instance of [[CS-E 130|CS-E 130(a)]] on fire.

## Compliance

- Fuel type and additive list, with the CS-E 560(a) declaration made for each type [AMC E 560(1)].
- Identification in the documentation of fuel specification parameters that could adversely affect the engine, such as sulphur or gum content [AMC E 560(1)].
- Corrosion and deterioration assessment of fuel system effects under [[CS-E 90]] [AMC E 560(1)].
- Contaminant definition covering aircraft-delivered contaminants and engine fuel system wear debris [AMC E 560(2)].
- Fuel system anti-icing means, with its mode of operation stated as continuous or automatic [AMC E 560(3)].
- Transient fuel icing threat assessment, or a declaration that no capability has been demonstrated, with the limitations, assumptions, ice quantity and critical fuel temperature documented in the engine installation manual [AMC E 560(4)].
- Blockage and bypass activation evidence at minimum fuel heating conditions and worst-case engine-to-engine variability [AMC E 560(4)].
- Incorrect assembly precautions for fuel system components, pipes and fittings, particularly those removed in routine maintenance [AMC E 560(5)], against [[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(d)]].
- Filter and strainer mounting arrangement with strength margins where the lines carry the weight [AMC E 560(6)], against [[CS-E 130|CS-E 130(a)]].
- Filter and strainer servicing provisions: accessibility, easily removable element, sediment trap and drain [AMC E 560(7)].
- Bypass operation restrictions specified in the manuals [AMC E 560(8)].
- Design precautions protecting fuel control settings against vibration, incorrect maintenance, mechanical interference and handling [AMC E 560(9)].

## Application to this engine

The AMC applies in full. Nothing in it is restricted by rating or control system.

**Transient fuel icing is the item to plan for.** The ice comes from the aircraft
fuel system, and the assessment belongs to the aircraft manufacturer. Because the
rotorcraft installation is not fixed, no completed threat assessment exists, so
point (4) puts the applicant to a choice now: assess the potential threat, or
declare that no capability has been demonstrated and carry that as an
installation limitation. Either way the ice quantity and the critical fuel
temperature must reach the engine installation manual under
[[CS-E 30|CS-E 30(a)]] and [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].

[VERIFY: whether a transient fuel icing threat assessment exists for the intended
rotorcraft installation, or whether the applicant declares that no capability has
been demonstrated. AMC E 560(4) requires one or the other, and the choice becomes
an installation limitation.]

**Point (9) reaches the EECS.** The likely causes of setting change it names —
vibration, incorrect maintenance, mechanical interference when installed or
during handling — apply to the fuel metering hardware, and the maintenance error
case connects to [[AMC E 510|AMC E 510(3)(h)]].

**Point (5) is a fire case, not only an assembly case.** It reasons from fuel
leakage being a potential fire hazard, so it ties [[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(d)]] to
[[CS-E 130]] and to the incorrect fitment assessment that
[[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(e)]] places inside the system safety assessment of
[[CS-E 50|CS-E 50(d)]].

## Not applicable

- **(1)**, in part — the interpretation extending the word "fuel" to other fluids such as water methanol. `engine_profile.md` declares no refrigerant injection and no boost fluid, so no such fluid arises on this engine. The multiple fuel type provision in the same point applies.
