# CS-E 100 — Strength

> [!summary]
> Maximum engine stresses must stay within values established by satisfactory
> practice for the material, taking account of the form of construction and the
> most severe operating conditions. A new material needs evidence for its assumed
> characteristics, and a turbine engine must address residual stresses in Engine
> Critical Parts. The mounting and any critically affected parts must carry the
> aircraft flight and ground loads together with local engine loads, and the
> engine must run across its declared flight envelope and speed and power range
> without excessive stress from vibration or excessive vibration into the
> airframe.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Keep the maximum stresses developed in the engine within values conforming to those established by satisfactory practice for the material involved, with due account of the particular form of construction and the most severe operating conditions. | Required |
| **(a)** | Where a new type of material is involved, have evidence available to substantiate the assumed material characteristics. | Required |
| **(a)** | For turbine engines, give due consideration to the effects of any residual stresses in Engine Critical Parts. | Required |
| **(b)** | Give the engine components forming part of the engine mounting, and any other engine parts liable to be critically affected, sufficient strength to withstand the aircraft flight and ground loads combined with the local loads arising from engine operation, when the engine is properly supported by a suitable engine-mounting structure. | Required |
| **(c)** | Design and construct the engine to function throughout its declared flight envelope and operating range of rotational speeds and power, without inducing excessive stress in any engine part because of vibration, and without imparting excessive vibration forces to the aircraft structure. | Required |

The residual-stress sentence in (a) is written for turbine engines specifically:
"For Turbine Engines, due consideration must be given to the effects of any
residual stresses in Engine Critical Parts" [CS-E 100(a)]. It therefore binds
this engine directly, and it ties CS-E 100 to the Engine Critical Part regime of
[[CS-E 515]].

Sub-point (b) sets a two-sided load case. The engine side supplies the local
loads from engine operation; the aircraft side supplies flight and ground loads
for the aircraft as a whole. The aircraft loads are an assumed installation
condition, so they are declared under [[CS-E 30|CS-E 30(a)]].

Sub-point (c) covers both directions of vibration: stress induced **in** the
engine, and vibration forces imparted **to** the aircraft structure. The second
is an installation interface and reaches the instructions for installation.

## Compliance

- Stress analysis against material allowables derived from satisfactory practice, covering the most severe operating conditions and the form of construction [CS-E 100(a)], using the design values established under [[CS-E 70|CS-E 70(a)]].
- Substantiation of assumed characteristics for any new type of material [CS-E 100(a)].
- Residual stress assessment for Engine Critical Parts [CS-E 100(a)], within the Engine Critical Part work of [[CS-E 515]].
- Mounting and critical-part strength analysis for combined aircraft and engine loads [CS-E 100(b)], against the installation assumptions declared under [[CS-E 30]].
- Vibration substantiation across the declared flight envelope and the operating range of rotational speeds and power [CS-E 100(c)], demonstrated by the vibration surveys of [[CS-E 650]].
- Declared vibration forces imparted to the aircraft structure, stated in the engine instructions for installation [CS-E 100(c)], within the manuals required by [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].

## Application to this engine

All three sub-points apply in full. The turbine-engine residual-stress sentence
in (a) is the one part written for this engine type rather than for engines
generally.

Sub-point (c) names the "operating range of rotational speeds and power/thrust"
[CS-E 100(c)]. For a turboshaft the thrust term is inapplicable and the power
term governs. The range is set by the declared ratings in `engine_profile.md`,
which include the OEI ratings: 30-Second OEI, 2-Minute OEI and Continuous OEI are
part of the declared operating range, so the vibration substantiation must cover
them and not only the all-engines-operating ratings.

CS-E 100 states the strength specification in Subpart A. The turbine-specific
strength substantiation, including the load cases and the integrity
demonstrations, is specified separately in [[CS-E 520]], and the two must be
read together.

## Not applicable

- Throughout — where the source pairs thrust with power, in any of the forms it uses, only the power term is carried. This engine produces shaft power; the thrust half of each pair has no turboshaft case.
