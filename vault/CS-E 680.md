# CS-E 680 — Inclination and Gyroscopic Load Effects

> [!summary]
> Two effects must be demonstrated. Inclination must not be seriously
> detrimental to engine running, and the engine must be designed to withstand
> the gyroscopic loads that normal flight manoeuvres produce. The paragraph
> names no angles, no load factors and no test method; the demonstration is
> normally made during flight testing.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Demonstrate that the effects of inclination on engine running are not seriously detrimental. | Required |
| — | Demonstrate that the engine is designed to withstand the gyroscopic loads resulting from normal flight manoeuvres. | Required |

CS-E 680 is a single unnumbered sentence, so the `Ref` cells carry no sub-point
identifier.

The two limbs test different things [CS-E 680]. Inclination is about
**running** — whether the engine continues to function when it is not level.
Gyroscopic load is about **structure** — whether the engine withstands the
moments that manoeuvring imposes on a spinning rotor. A demonstration of one
does not serve the other.

Neither limb carries a number [CS-E 680]. The inclination angles and the
manoeuvre set come from the intended installation, so both are installation
assumptions under [[CS-E 30]].

"Normal flight manoeuvres" bounds the gyroscopic case [CS-E 680]. Manoeuvres
outside the normal set are not covered here.

## Compliance

- Demonstration that inclination is not seriously detrimental to engine running, across the intended inclination range [CS-E 680]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 680]] — normally proved during flight testing.
- Demonstration that the engine withstands gyroscopic loads from normal flight manoeuvres [CS-E 680]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 680]].
- Supplementary running or rig testing to an agreed schedule, where required before flight tests can be permitted [AMC E 680]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 680]].
- Statement of the inclination range and the normal flight manoeuvre set assumed, declared under [[CS-E 30|CS-E 30(a)]] and carried into the instructions for installation of [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies [CS-E 680], and it carries more weight on a rotorcraft
than the short wording suggests.

The inclination limb connects directly to [[CS-E 600|CS-E 600(e)]], which
requires tests to be made normally with the engine mounted in the attitude in
which it will be installed. It also connects to [[AMC E 600|AMC E 600(e)]],
under which the applicant should justify any difference from the attitude in the
intended rotorcraft installation. The system most exposed is the oil system:
[[CS-E 570|CS-E 570(a)(1)]] requires proper functioning "under all intended
flight attitudes", which is the same question CS-E 680 asks from the test side.

The gyroscopic limb depends on the rotorcraft's manoeuvre envelope. The loads it
produces combine with the mounting loads of [[CS-E 100|CS-E 100(b)]]. That
sub-point requires the engine mounting and critically affected parts to
withstand aircraft flight and ground loads together with the local loads from
engine operation. [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(3)]] treats flight manoeuvre loads the
same way for pressure-loaded parts: analyse them, and test under them where
they are significant.

[VERIFY: the inclination range and the normal flight manoeuvre set for the
intended rotorcraft installation are not recorded in `engine_profile.md`. Both
bound the CS-E 680 demonstration, and both are installation assumptions under
CS-E 30.]
