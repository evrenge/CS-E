# CS-E 840 — Rotor Integrity

> [!summary]
> Every compressor and turbine rotor, built to the most adverse combination of
> material properties and dimensional tolerances its type design allows, must not
> burst when run for five minutes at whichever of four defined over-speed
> conditions is most critical. Two of the four are rating-based margins — 120 %
> generally, 115 % for OEI ratings under 2½ minutes — and two derive from the
> highest speed a Failure could produce. A separate demonstration covers rotor
> growth at 100 % of the most critical Failure speed.

## Requirement

### Burst

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | For each compressor and turbine rotor, establish by test, analysis, or combination thereof, that a rotor which has the most adverse combination of material properties and dimensional tolerances allowed by its type design will not burst when it is operated in the engine for five minutes at whichever of the conditions defined in CS-E 840(b) is the most critical with respect to the integrity of such a rotor. | Required |
| **(a)** | Where that required condition is determined by CS-E 840(b)(3) or (b)(4), but the associated Failure condition is of a sudden transient nature such as loss of load, and it precludes any further operation of the affected rotor, the time period of that Failure condition is an acceptable duration for showing compliance by means of an engine test, provided the required test speeds are achieved. | Relief |
| **(a)** | Test rotors which do not have the most adverse combination of material properties and dimensional tolerances must comply at appropriately adjusted test parameters, for example speed, temperature and loads. | Required |

### The four speed conditions

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(b)** | When determining the operating conditions applicable to each rotor for compliance with CS-E 840(a) and (c), evaluate each of the following speeds in conjunction with their associated temperatures and temperature gradients, throughout the engine's operating envelope. | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | 120 % of the maximum permissible rotor speeds associated with any of the ratings except OEI ratings of less than 2½-minutes. | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | 115 % of the maximum permissible rotor speeds associated with any OEI ratings of less than 2½-minutes. | Required |
| **(b)(3)(i)** | 105 % of the highest rotor speed that would result from the Failure of the component or system which, in a representative installation of the engine, is the most critical with respect to over-speeding when operating at any rating condition except OEI ratings of less than 2½-minutes. | Required |
| **(b)(3)(ii)** | …or 105 % of the highest rotor speed resulting from the Failure of any component or system in a representative installation, in combination with any other Failure that would not normally be detected during a routine pre-flight check or during normal flight operation, that is the most critical with respect to over-speeding, except as provided by CS-E 840(c), when operating at any rating condition except OEI ratings of less than 2½-minutes. | Required |
| **(b)(4)** | 100 % of the highest rotor speed that would result from the Failure of the component or system which, in a representative installation of the engine, is the most critical with respect to over-speeding when operating at any OEI ratings of less than 2½-minutes. | Required |
| **(c)** | Include the highest over-speed which will result from a complete loss of load on a turbine rotor in the over-speeds considered under each of CS-E 840(b)(3)(i), (ii) and (b)(4), irrespective of whether it is the result of a Failure within the engine or external to the engine — unless it can be shown to be Extremely Remote under the provisions of CS-E 850. | Required |
| **(c)** | Consider over-speeds resulting from any other single Failure. Consider over-speeds resulting from multiple Failures also, unless they can be shown to be Extremely Remote. | Required |

The four conditions fall into two pairs, and the OEI boundary runs between them.
Conditions (b)(1) and (b)(2) are margins on **rating** speeds; (b)(3) and (b)(4)
are margins on **Failure** speeds. In both pairs, the OEI ratings of less than
2½ minutes are separated out and given the lower factor. That factor is 115 %
instead of 120 %, and 100 % instead of 105 %.

Neither CS-E 840 nor AMC E 840 gives a reason for the split; the boundary is
stated, not explained. The engine must be evaluated at all four conditions and
tested at whichever proves most critical.

Sub-point (c) makes loss of load a mandatory inclusion rather than one Failure
among many, with a single escape: showing it Extremely Remote under
[[CS-E 850]].

### Growth

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)** | In addition, for each compressor and turbine rotor, establish by test, analysis, or combination thereof, that a rotor with the most adverse combination of material properties and dimensional tolerances allowed by its type design, operated in the engine for five minutes at 100 % of the most critical speed and temperature conditions resulting from any Failure or combination of Failures considered under CS-E 840(b)(3) and (b)(4), will meet the acceptance criteria of (d)(1) and (d)(2). | Required |
| **(d)** | Where the Failure condition is of a sudden transient nature such as loss of load, and it precludes any further operation of the affected rotor, the time period of that Failure condition is an acceptable duration for showing compliance by means of an engine test. | Relief |
| **(d)(1)(i)** | Growth of the rotor while operating at the applicable conditions must not cause the engine to catch fire. | Required |
| **(d)(1)(ii)** | …or to release high energy debris through the engine's casing, or result in a hazardous Failure of the engine's casing. | Required |
| **(d)(1)(iii)** | …or to generate loads greater than those ultimate loads for which the engine's mountings have been designed in compliance with CS-E 100(b). | Required |
| **(d)(1)(iv)** | …or to lose the capability of being shut down. | Required |
| **(d)(2)** | After the applicable period of operation, the rotor must not exhibit conditions such as cracking or distortion which preclude the safe operation of the engine during any likely continued operation following such an over-speed event in service. | Required |

Sub-point (d) is a second demonstration, not a restatement of (a). Burst is
absence of rupture; growth is whether the rotor's radial expansion causes
secondary damage. [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(2)(j)]] notes that the most critical
rotor for burst might not be the most critical for growth.

The four consequences in (d)(1) are cumulative prohibitions. Item (iii) links the
rotor directly to the mounting design of [[CS-E 100|CS-E 100(b)]], and item (iv)
to the Hazardous Engine Effect of [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(g)(2)(vii)]].

## Compliance

- Analysis submitted to the Agency determining which of the four CS-E 840(b) conditions is most critical for each rotor stage, separately for (a) and for (d) [CS-E 840(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(2)(d)]].
- Burst demonstration by test, analysis or combination at the most critical condition, for five minutes or for the transient Failure duration where the relief applies [CS-E 840(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(3)]].
- Growth demonstration at 100 % of the most critical Failure speed and temperature, against the four consequences and the post-test condition [CS-E 840(d)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(2)(h)]], [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(2)(i)]].
- Loss-of-load over-speed determination, considering system inertia, available gas energy, whether the rotor is held in plane, and over-speed protection devices [CS-E 840(c)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(5)]].
- Extremely Remote demonstration under [[CS-E 850]] where loss of load is excluded [CS-E 840(c)].
- Burst speed of each rotor design established and reported in the certification documentation, on the most adverse combination of tolerances and material properties [CS-E 840(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(2)(k)]].
- Rotor temperature and temperature gradient survey on an engine, or validated analysis, with test speed or blade mass adjusted for any deviation [CS-E 840(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(4)(a)]].
- Sample rotor material property determination and dimensional tolerance analysis [CS-E 840(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(4)(b)]], [[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(4)(c)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies to every compressor and turbine rotor.

**Two of the four conditions are written around the short OEI ratings.**
`engine_profile.md` declares 30-Second OEI and 2-Minute OEI, both of less than
2½ minutes. Therefore, (b)(2) and (b)(4) apply to them, and (b)(1) and (b)(3)
apply to every other rating. Continuous OEI is not "less than 2½-minutes", so
it falls under the 120 % and 105 % factors with Take-off Power, Maximum
Continuous Power and Rated 30-Minute Power.

| Rating | Duration | Rating margin | Failure margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum Continuous Power | continuous | **(b)(1)** 120 % | **(b)(3)** 105 % |
| Rated 30-Minute Power | 30 min | **(b)(1)** 120 % | **(b)(3)** 105 % |
| Take-off Power | 5 min | **(b)(1)** 120 % | **(b)(3)** 105 % |
| Continuous OEI | continuous | **(b)(1)** 120 % | **(b)(3)** 105 % |
| 2-Minute OEI | 2 min | **(b)(2)** 115 % | **(b)(4)** 100 % |
| 30-Second OEI | 30 s | **(b)(2)** 115 % | **(b)(4)** 100 % |

This is the third boundary in the vault that separates the short OEI ratings from
the rest, and it is drawn at 2½ minutes rather than at two. [[CS-E 650|CS-E 650(b)]]
draws its line at "two minutes or longer" and [[AMC E 730|AMC E 730(3)]] at "more
than 2 minutes"; here the line is "less than 2½-minutes". The 2-Minute OEI rating
falls on the short side of this one.

**Loss of load is the governing Failure for a free power turbine.** A complete
loss of load on a turbine rotor must be included under (c) unless shown
Extremely Remote under [[CS-E 850]]. For a free power-turbine engine, the load
path runs through the output shaft to the rotorcraft transmission. Therefore, a
loss of load originates outside the engine as readily as inside it. Sub-point
(c) says the origin does not matter.

[VERIFY: whether a complete loss of load on the power turbine can be shown
Extremely Remote under CS-E 850, taking account of the aircraft-side transmission
and any gearbox supplied by the aircraft manufacturer.
[[AMC E 850|AMC E 850(2)(c)]] requires the loss of loads caused by a Failure of
any gearbox supplied by the aircraft manufacturer to be identified in the
analysis.]

**Blade shedding interacts with the speed factors.**
[[AMC E 840|AMC E 840(2)(d)]] states that where the peak over-speed is limited
by deliberate blade shedding, the (b)(3) and (b)(4) factors still apply to a
fully bladed rotor at the shedding speed. It also states that the most
critical speed for rotor integrity might not coincide with the highest
achievable shedding speed. That connects to [[CS-E 810|CS-E 810(b)]], where
shedding relied on for over-speed protection must sit above the maximum
approved speed and below the minimum rotor burst speed.

## Not applicable

- **(a)**, **(d)**, in part — the fan rotor, named alongside compressor and turbine rotors throughout. A turboshaft has no fan; the compressor and turbine rotor cases apply in full.
