# CS-E 560 — Fuel System

> [!summary]
> Each approved fuel specification, its additives and its flow, temperature and
> pressure limits must be declared and substantiated, and the fuel pump must
> carry a margin over maximum engine demand. Filtration must handle the likely
> contaminant load, including water, with impending blockage indicated to the
> flight crew or to maintenance. A by-pass must keep fuel flowing without
> releasing collected contaminants or causing a Hazardous Engine Effect. Water
> separation and fuel system icing must not need flight crew action.

## Requirement

### Fuel specification and pump capacity

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | Declare and substantiate each fuel specification to be approved, including any additive, and the associated limitations in flow, temperature and pressure that ensure proper engine functioning under all intended operating conditions. | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Identify any parameter of the fuel specification likely to adversely affect engine functioning or durability, so that engine or rig testing using appropriate fuel may be conducted where necessary. | Required |
| **(a)(3)** | Give the engine fuel pump a margin of capacity over the maximum engine demand in the flight envelope, consistent with the assumed aircraft installation specifications. | Required |

### Filtration and blockage indication

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(b)(1)** | Provide filters, strainers or other equivalent means to protect the fuel system from malfunction due to contaminants. | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | Give those devices the capacity to accommodate any likely quantity of contaminants, including water, in relation to recommended servicing intervals and, if provided, the blockage or by-pass indication system. | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | Provide a means to permit indication of impending blockage of any main fuel filter or strainer located between the engine fuel inlet and any device having a significant function for the control of the power. | Required |
| **(b)(2)(i)** | One option is to give that indication to the flight crew. | Required |
| **(b)(2)(ii)** | The other option is to give it to the maintenance crew, if it can be shown that the engine will continue to operate normally with the levels of contamination specified, for a period equal to the inspection interval of the impending blockage indicator. | Required |

### By-pass

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(c)** | Where a by-pass means is provided on any filter or strainer, design it so that, if the filter or strainer element is completely blocked, fuel will continue to flow at an acceptable rate through the rest of the system. | Required |
| **(c)(1)** | Design the by-pass so that, when it is in operation, the previously collected contaminants in the filter or strainer will not enter the engine fuel system downstream of the filter or strainer. | Required |
| **(c)(2)** | Design the fuel system so that, when the by-pass is open, operation on contaminated fuel does not result in a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Required |
| **(c)(3)** | Provide indication of by-pass operation, if the maintenance action to be taken after by-pass operation differs from that following an indication of impending blockage. | Required |

### Water, icing and integrity

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)** | Design the fuel system so that any accumulation of likely quantities of water which may separate from the fuel will not cause engine malfunctioning. | Required |
| **(e)** | Where icing can occur in the fuel system, ensure continued satisfactory functioning of the engine in such circumstances without the need for any action by the flight crew. | Required |
| **(e)** | Where compliance relies upon fuel anti-icing additives or other means incorporated in the aircraft fuel system, declare this under CS-E 30 together with a statement of the conditions which must be met. | Required |
| **(f)** | Make provision near each fuel pressure connection provided for instrumentation so as to limit the loss of fluid in the event of a pipe Failure. | Required |
| **(g)** | Take design precautions against the possibility of errors and inadvertent or unauthorised changes in setting of all fuel control adjusting means. | Required |

Sub-point (b)(2) offers a choice of recipient, not a choice of whether to
indicate. Routing the indication to maintenance instead of the flight crew costs
a demonstration. Normal engine operation must be shown at the specified
contamination levels, for a period equal to the indicator's inspection interval
[CS-E 560(b)(2)(ii)].

Sub-point (e) forbids reliance on the flight crew, but permits reliance on the
aircraft. Anti-icing additives or aircraft fuel system means are allowed, on
condition of a declaration under [[CS-E 30]] and a statement of the conditions
that must be met [CS-E 560(e)]. Those conditions then form part of the declared
installation assumptions.

The by-pass provisions in (c) are cumulative with the main sentence. A by-pass
must keep fuel flowing, must not release what the filter already caught, must not
lead to a Hazardous Engine Effect, and must be indicated where it changes the
maintenance action [CS-E 560(c)].

## Compliance

- Declared fuel specifications with additives, and their flow, temperature and pressure limitations, with substantiation [CS-E 560(a)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(1)]].
- Identification of fuel specification parameters affecting functioning or durability, with engine or rig testing on appropriate fuel where necessary [CS-E 560(a)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(1)]].
- Fuel pump capacity margin over maximum engine demand across the flight envelope, against the assumed installation specifications of [[CS-E 30]] [CS-E 560(a)(3)].
- Filtration capacity substantiation against the likely contaminant load including water, tied to the recommended servicing intervals [CS-E 560(b)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(2)]]; contaminated fuel testing is [[CS-E 670]].
- Impending blockage indication design, with the normal-operation demonstration where the indication goes to maintenance rather than the flight crew [CS-E 560(b)(2)].
- By-pass design substantiation covering flow rate, contaminant retention, absence of Hazardous Engine Effect on contaminated fuel, and by-pass operation indication [CS-E 560(c)]. Any restriction in by-pass operation condition is specified in the manuals [AMC E 560(8)].
- Water accumulation assessment [CS-E 560(d)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(4)]].
- Fuel system icing substantiation without flight crew action, with the transient fuel icing threat assessment and its limitations documented in the engine installation manual [CS-E 560(e)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(3)]], [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(4)]].
- Declaration under [[CS-E 30]] where compliance relies on anti-icing additives or aircraft fuel system means, with the conditions to be met [CS-E 560(e)].
- Fluid loss limitation provisions at fuel pressure instrumentation connections [CS-E 560(f)].
- Design precautions against error and unauthorised change in fuel control settings [CS-E 560(g)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(9)]].

## Application to this engine

All sub-points apply. None is restricted by engine type or rating.

Sub-point (a)(3) is bounded by the declared ratings. The fuel pump margin is
"over the maximum Engine demand in the flight envelope" [CS-E 560(a)(3)], and the
declared ratings are what define that envelope of demand.

[VERIFY: which declared rating produces the maximum engine fuel demand.
CS-E 560(a)(3) does not say that the maximum demand occurs at the highest rating,
CS-E 40 establishes no ordering of power between the OEI ratings, and
`engine_profile.md` records rating names without power values. The pump capacity
case cannot be assigned to a rating from the sources held.]

Sub-point (b)(2) refers to a device "having a significant function for the
control of the thrust or power" [CS-E 560(b)(2)]. For a turboshaft the power
term governs, and with a full-authority EECS the fuel metering unit is that
device, so the main fuel filter upstream of it falls under (b)(2).

Sub-point (g) reaches the control system. Fuel control adjusting means on an
EECS-equipped engine include both the hydromechanical settings and any adjustable
control system data. Therefore, the design precautions of
[[AMC E 560|AMC E 560(9)]] — locking devices, sealing, inaccessible installation
— apply alongside the protection specifications of [[CS-E 50]].

The fuel system icing item is also listed "As a reminder" in Table 1 of
[[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(a)]]. That entry points back to CS-E 560(e), rather than
adding a test of its own.

## Not applicable

- **(b)(2)**, in part — the thrust term in "the control of the thrust or power". This engine produces shaft power.
