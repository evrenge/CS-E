# CS-E 770 — Low Temperature Starting Tests

> [!summary]
> The engine must start and accelerate satisfactorily from the minimum
> temperatures the constructor declares, and the declared temperature for service
> use is normally the oil temperature. Two separate demonstrations follow: that
> starting is feasible and harmless with the carcass and oil at the declared
> minimum, using both the minimum and maximum declared starting torques; and that
> a one-second acceleration to take-off is smooth and harmless with the oil at
> its declared minimum for selecting Take-off Power.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Show the engine capable of satisfactory starting and acceleration from the appropriate minimum temperatures declared by the constructor, demonstrated as indicated in CS-E 770(b) and (c). | Required |
| **(a)** | Unless otherwise agreed, the temperature indicated for service use should be the oil temperature. | Recommended |
| **(b)** | Provide evidence that engine starting with the engine carcass and oil at the declared minimum temperature, using the minimum and maximum starting torques declared for service use, is feasible and will not damage the engine. | Required |
| **(b)** | If a non-standard starting procedure is necessary below a certain temperature, establish that procedure and quote the relevant details in the engine operating instructions, in addition to the standard procedure. | Required |
| **(c)** | Provide evidence that, with the engine oil at the declared minimum temperature for the selection of Take-off Power, smooth acceleration of the engine is obtained without engine damage when the power control is moved from minimum test bed idle to the position appropriate to take-off in a time not greater than one second. | Required |

Two temperatures are declared and they are not the same. Sub-point (b) uses the
minimum carcass and oil temperature for **starting**; sub-point (c) uses the
minimum oil temperature for **selecting Take-off Power**. An engine may be
startable at a temperature at which take-off power may not yet be selected, and
the two limits are declared separately.

The verb in the second row of (a) is "should", inside a CS paragraph, so the
strength is Recommended rather than Required. The oil temperature is the normal
service indication but another basis may be agreed.

Sub-point (b) tests both ends of the starter torque range. A start that succeeds
at maximum torque does not evidence the minimum-torque case.

The one-second acceleration in (c) is the same step that
[[CS-E 745|CS-E 745(a)(2)]] requires at normal temperature, so (c) is that
demonstration repeated at the cold limit.

## Compliance

- Declared minimum engine carcass and oil temperature for starting [CS-E 770(b)].
- Declared minimum oil temperature for selection of Take-off Power [CS-E 770(c)].
- Declared minimum and maximum starting torques for service use [CS-E 770(b)], consistent with the starter provisions of [[CS-E 590]].
- Evidence that starting at the declared minimum temperature, at both torque extremes, is feasible and does not damage the engine [CS-E 770(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 770]].
- Non-standard starting procedure below any temperature where one is necessary, quoted in the engine operating instructions of [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]] alongside the standard procedure [CS-E 770(b)].
- Evidence of smooth acceleration without damage from minimum test bed idle to take-off in not more than one second, at the declared minimum oil temperature [CS-E 770(c)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 770]].
- Any declared minimum oil temperature for opening up from ground idle for warming up or taxying [CS-E 770]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 770]].

## Application to this engine

All sub-points apply. Nothing here is restricted by rating.

The declared temperatures are installation-facing. They become operating
limitations under [[CS-E 40|CS-E 40(d)]] and installation information under
[[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]], and they bound the fuel and oil limits established by
[[CS-E 660]] and the oil system functioning required by
[[CS-E 570|CS-E 570(a)(1)]].

[[AMC E 770]] gives two reliefs that shape the test. The engine may normally
inspire uncooled intake air in both tests, so only the carcass and oil need to be
cold, not the whole test cell. And the constructor may declare a separate minimum
oil temperature, with associated conditions, for opening up from ground idle to
warm up or taxi — a third temperature, below the (c) limit for take-off
selection.

[VERIFY: the declared minimum engine carcass and oil temperature for starting,
the declared minimum oil temperature for selecting Take-off Power, and the
declared minimum and maximum starting torques are not recorded in
`engine_profile.md`. All four are inputs to the CS-E 770 demonstrations.]

The cold-start case also appears in [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(3)(d)(i)]], which
requires the impact of cold and hot engine starts to be considered in the Engine
Flight Cycle used to establish the Approved Life, and in
[[CS-E 740|CS-E 740(f)(4)(v)]], where an acceleration from cold may produce a
transient over-temperature for which a 2-minute EGT limit may be approved.

## Not applicable

- Throughout — "thrust or power" and its variants are rendered as power. This engine produces shaft power; the thrust half of each pair has no turboshaft case.
- **(c)**, in part — the aeroplane power or thrust control lever wording. The rotorcraft parenthesis, minimum test bed idle, is what applies and is carried.
