# CS-E 930 — Initial Maintenance Programme Test

> [!summary]
> The paragraph is new at Amendment 8. A test must be performed on an engine
> that adequately conforms to the type design. The test substantiates the
> initial maintenance programme (IMP) that will be included in the instructions
> for continued airworthiness (ICA). It must simulate the conditions in which
> the engine is expected to operate in service, including typical start–stop
> cycles. The paragraph sets no cycle count and no duration.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Perform a test with an engine that adequately conforms to the type design, to substantiate the initial maintenance programme (IMP) that will be included in the instructions for continued airworthiness (ICA), in order to ensure sufficient engine reliability under in-service conditions. | Required |
| — | The test must simulate the conditions in which the engine is expected to operate in service, including typical start–stop cycles. | Required |

CS-E 930 has no sub-points. Every parameter of the test is left to
[[AMC E 930]]: the cycle type, the engine build, the duration, the pass criteria
and what credit the result carries into the maintenance programme.

Three phrases carry the whole paragraph.

**"adequately conforms to the type design"** — not a development engine.
[[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(2)(i)]] reads this as meaning that no significant engine
modification should be required to complete the test.

**"simulate the conditions in which the Engine is expected to operate in
service"** — the test cycle must be justified against the predicted service
usage, which [[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(a)(1)]] names the Engine flight cycle.

**"including typical start–stop cycles"** — the start and shutdown are inside the
test, not a means of getting the engine to the test condition. This is what
separates the IMP test from the endurance test of [[CS-E 740]].

## Compliance

- A test plan with an assessment of expected service operating conditions, showing that the proposed test cycle represents the expected in-service engine flight cycles [CS-E 930]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(1)]].
- A test engine conforming to the type design, with any uninstalled type-design parts substantiated by validated analysis or other tests [CS-E 930]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(2)]].
- The test itself, of a duration and cycle count correlated to the planned IMP [CS-E 930]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(4)]].
- Post-test teardown inspection findings [CS-E 930]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(5)(iii)]].
- The initial maintenance programme itself, delivered as part of the ICA under [[CS-E 25]], with life limits, inspections, intervals and accept/reject criteria [CS-E 930]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(5)(iv)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies and is new work. No equivalent existed at Amendment 7, so
it adds a test and a substantiation package to the certification programme rather
than refining one.

**The turboshaft case is written into the AMC.**
[[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(2)(v)]] asks the applicant to load the test engine
output shaft so as to simulate the rotor drive system characteristics of the
intended installation. It names inertial and torsional vibration. The IMP test
therefore cannot be run on a bare dynamometer load without justification, and
the test rig depends on the target rotorcraft transmission.

**OEI usage is part of the cycle.**
Where the engine includes OEI power ratings, the engine flight cycles of the
IMP test should represent the cumulative usage of those ratings before
maintenance [AMC E 930(d)(1)(i)]. This engine declares three, so the test cycle
carries a representative OEI usage assumption.

**The IMP test result can serve CS-E 25(b)(2).**
[[AMC E 930|AMC E 930(d)(5)(iii)]] states that for engines having 30-second OEI
and 2-minute OEI power ratings, the IMP test results may be used when showing
compliance with [[CS-E 25|CS-E 25(b)(2)]]. That compliance is for OEI power
availability demonstration at the end of the fixed engine overhaul period. This
is a direct saving for this engine and is worth planning for before the test
rather than claiming after it.

**It is not the endurance test.**
[[CS-E 740]] substantiates the engine against its ratings. CS-E 930 substantiates
the maintenance programme against expected service usage. The cycles differ, the
duration basis differs, and the pass criteria differ.

## Amendment history

CS-E 930 is **new at Amendment 8**. The Change Information declares it as "The
following CS-E 930 is added:" and no text was deleted, because no paragraph
CS-E 930 existed in Amendment 7. [work/redline/CS_E_930_Amdt8.md]

The obligation is wholly new: an applicant certifying to Amendment 7 ran no IMP
test, because the paragraph did not exist. What such an applicant did instead is
not stated in any source held here.
