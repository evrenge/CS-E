# CS-E 790 — Ingestion of Rain and Hail

> [!summary]
> Two ingestion cases apply to every engine. Large hailstones aimed at the inlet
> at rough-air true airspeed must not cause unacceptable damage or power loss or
> require a shutdown, with the stone count fixed by inlet throat area. Separately,
> the engine must operate acceptably in the certification standard concentrations
> of Appendix A. For a rotorcraft turbine engine, CS-E 790(b) offers an
> alternative to that second case: a static ground-level rain ingestion test at
> 4 percent water-to-air by weight.

## Requirement

### Large hailstone ingestion

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | The ingestion of large hailstones of 0.8 to 0.9 specific gravity, at the maximum true airspeed for altitudes up to 4 500 metres associated with a representative aircraft operating in rough air, with the engine at Maximum Continuous Power, must not cause unacceptable mechanical damage or unacceptable power loss after the ingestion, or require the engine to be shut down. | Required |
| **(a)(1)** | Perform engine tests, unless it is agreed that alternative evidence can be used — such as results from other engine tests, rig tests, analysis, or an appropriate combination — provided by the applicant from their experience with engines of comparable size, design, construction, performance and handling characteristics, obtained during development, certification or operation. | Required |
| **(a)(1)** | Aim one-half the number of hailstones randomly over the inlet face area and the other half at the critical inlet face area, and ingest them in a rapid sequence to simulate a hailstone encounter. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(i)** | One 25-millimetres diameter hailstone for engines with inlet throat areas of not more than 0.0645 m². | Required |
| **(a)(1)(ii)** | One 25-millimetres diameter and one 50-millimetres diameter hailstone for each 0.0968 m² of inlet throat area, or fraction thereof, for engines with inlet throat areas of more than 0.0645 m². | Required |

### Certification standard concentrations, and the rotorcraft alternative

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(2)** | In addition to complying with CS-E 790(a)(1), and except as provided in CS-E 790(b), show that each engine is capable of acceptable operation throughout its specified operating envelope when subjected to sudden encounters with the certification standard concentrations of rain and hail as defined in Appendix A to CS-E. | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Acceptable engine operation precludes, during any 3-Minute continuous period in rain and during any 30-Second continuous period in hail, the occurrence of flameout, rundown, continued or non-recoverable surge or stall, or loss of acceleration and deceleration capability. | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Show after the ingestion that there is no unacceptable mechanical damage, unacceptable power loss, or other adverse engine anomalies. | Required |
| **(b)** | Engines for Rotorcraft. As an alternative to CS-E 790(a)(2), but for rotorcraft turbine engines only, show that each engine is capable of acceptable operation during and after the ingestion of rain with an overall ratio of water droplet flow to airflow, by weight, with a uniform distribution at the inlet plane, of at least 4-percent. | Permitted |
| **(b)** | Acceptable engine operation precludes flameout, rundown, continued or non-recoverable surge or stall, or loss of acceleration and deceleration capability. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)** | Show after the ingestion that there is no unacceptable mechanical damage, unacceptable power loss, or other adverse engine anomalies. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(1)** | A normal stabilisation period at Take-off Power without rain ingestion, followed immediately by the suddenly commencing ingestion of rain for three minutes at Take-off Power. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(2)** | Continuation of the rain ingestion during subsequent rapid deceleration to minimum idle power. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(3)** | Continuation of the rain ingestion during three minutes at minimum idle power to be certified for flight operation. | Required if claimed |
| **(b)(4)** | Continuation of the rain ingestion during subsequent rapid acceleration to Take-off Power. | Required if claimed |

Sub-point (b) is an alternative to (a)(2) only. It does not displace (a)(1): the
large hailstone ingestion still applies, because (a) is headed "All Engines"
[CS-E 790(a)].

The four steps of (b) are one continuous test. Rain ingestion starts in (b)(1)
and continues without interruption through the deceleration, the idle period and
the acceleration, so the engine is wet throughout the transients.

The test is static and at ground level, which is why the concentration is raised:
4 percent water-to-air by weight replaces the altitude-varying Appendix A values.

### Protection device waiver

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)** | For an engine that incorporates or requires the use of a protection device, demonstration of the rain and hail ingestion capabilities required in CS-E 790(a) and (b) may be waived wholly or in part by the Agency if (d)(1), (d)(2) and (d)(3) are shown. | Relief |
| **(d)(1)** | The subject rain or hail constituents are of a size that will not pass through the protection device. | Required if claimed |
| **(d)(2)** | The protection device will withstand the impact of the subject rain or hail constituents. | Required if claimed |
| **(d)(3)** | The subject rain or hail constituents stopped by the protection device will not obstruct the flow of induction air into the engine, resulting in damage, power loss, or other adverse engine anomalies in excess of what would be accepted in CS-E 790(a) and (b). | Required if claimed |

The three conditions are cumulative, and the waiver is granted by the Agency
rather than claimed. It may be partial.

## Compliance

- Inlet throat area measurement, determining the hailstone count under (a)(1)(i) or (a)(1)(ii) [CS-E 790(a)(1)].
- Large hailstone ingestion test at the rough-air maximum true airspeed with the engine at Maximum Continuous Power, with half the stones aimed randomly and half at the critical inlet face area [CS-E 790(a)(1)].
- Alternative evidence in place of the hailstone test, where agreed, from comparable engines [CS-E 790(a)(1)]. Accepted means for design changes and derivative engines: [[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(a)(1)]].
- Static ground-level rain ingestion test at 4 percent water-to-air by weight, run as the continuous four-step sequence of (b) [CS-E 790(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(a)(2)(d)]].
- Interpretation of "unacceptable mechanical damage" and "unacceptable power loss" against the 3 percent sustained loss and 10 percent degradation criteria [CS-E 790(a)(1)], [CS-E 790(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(1)]].
- Engine performance measured before and after ingestion, normalised, across the full range of engine power [AMC E 790(a)(2)(5)(c)(vi)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(a)(2)(5)(c)(vi)]].
- Further running or other evidence where damage is found after test, to show that subsequent Failures resulting from the damage are unlikely to occur before the damage is rectified [AMC E 790(a)(2)(5)(c)(vi)].
- Protection device evidence against the three conditions, where a waiver is sought [CS-E 790(d)]. Where the device is supplied by the aircraft, the conditions are imposed on the installation through the instructions for installation [AMC E 790(5)].

## Application to this engine

**Sub-point (b) is the route for this engine, and the AMC says so.**
[[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(a)(2)(d)]] states that "For rotorcraft applications,
testing to the specifications of CS-E 790(a)(2) may be replaced by the static
rain ingestion test specified in CS-E 790(b)." It also gives the reasoning: the
proportionately higher engine power during descent and the lower flight speeds of
a rotorcraft produce a small scoop factor effect, and rotorcraft turbine engines
might not have rotating components that centrifuge rain or hail away from the
engine. Raising the ambient concentration to 4 percent water-to-air "will usually
compensate for any flight effects".

The strength of (b) is Permitted because it is an alternative the applicant may
elect. Once elected, its four steps and its acceptance criteria are mandatory,
which is why those rows read Required if claimed.

**Sub-point (a)(1) is not displaced.** The large hailstone ingestion applies to
all engines and is unaffected by the (b) election. The stone count depends on
inlet throat area, and a turboshaft inlet is small, so the (a)(1)(i) single
25-millimetre stone case may apply.

[VERIFY: the engine inlet throat area is not recorded in `engine_profile.md`. It
determines whether CS-E 790(a)(1)(i) or (a)(1)(ii) applies, and therefore the
number and size of hailstones to be ingested.]

**The transients in (b) are one-second movements.**
[[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(4)]] states that "rapid acceleration" and "rapid
deceleration" in CS-E 790(b)(2) and (b)(4) "should be interpreted as meaning a
throttle movement in not more than one second". That is the same one-second step
as [[CS-E 745|CS-E 745(a)(2)]], now performed with the engine ingesting rain.

**A protection device changes the picture.** If the rotorcraft installation
provides an inlet barrier filter or similar, [[CS-E 790|CS-E 790(d)]] may waive
the demonstration wholly or in part, but [[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(5)]] requires the
engine approval to be endorsed accordingly and the conditions of (d)(1) to (d)(3)
to be imposed on the installation through the instructions for installation.

[VERIFY: whether the intended rotorcraft installation incorporates or requires an
air intake protection device. It determines whether the CS-E 790(d) waiver is
available, and the same question arises for the air intake guard in
CS-E 780(e).]

**Automatic protection systems become dispatch-critical.**
[[AMC E 790|AMC E 790(a)(2)(5)(c)(vi)]] states that where compliance depends on
an automatic protection system such as continuous ignition, auto-relight or a
surge recovery system, "the availability of this system is considered to be
critical for dispatch". If continuous ignition under [[CS-E 720]] is relied on
here, that consequence follows.

The rain and hail results also feed [[CS-E 650]]:
[[AMC E 650|AMC E 650(5)]] permits the CS-E 790 test evidence to be used to
characterise engine vibration behaviour, and [[AMC E 650|AMC E 650(9)(b)]]
requires the stress margin criteria to account for rain and hail effects.

## Not applicable

- **(c)** — the separate supersonic cruise hailstone test for Engines for Supersonic Aeroplanes, with its 25 millimetres at 10 500 metres to 6 millimetres at 18 000 metres linear diameter variation. The declared application is a rotorcraft.
