# CS-E 910 — Relighting In Flight

> [!summary]
> The paragraph has two sentences: one sets the duty and one sets the content.
> The engine constructor, here the applicant, must recommend an envelope of
> conditions for relighting the engine in flight. It must substantiate the
> envelope by tests or other evidence. The recommendation must state every
> applicable condition, and the paragraph names five by example.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Recommend an envelope of conditions for engine relighting in flight. | Required |
| — | Substantiate the recommended envelope by appropriate tests or other evidence. | Required |
| — | State all the conditions applicable in the recommendation, for example altitude, air speed, engine windmilling rotational speed, whether starter assistance is required, and the recommended drill. | Required |

CS-E 910 has no sub-points. The five conditions are introduced by "e.g.", so the
list is illustrative and not a closed set. The obligation is to state "all the
conditions applicable" [CS-E 910]. Each of the five that applies to this engine
is stated, and so is any other applicable condition.

The verb is "recommend", but the duty is mandatory: the engine constructor must
recommend an envelope and must substantiate it [CS-E 910]. The envelope is
advice to the aircraft; producing and substantiating it is not optional.

## Compliance

- A declared relight envelope, with altitude and air speed boundaries [CS-E 910].
- Engine windmilling rotational speed, where it is an applicable condition [CS-E 910].
- A statement of whether starter assistance is required, and where [CS-E 910].
- The recommended relight drill [CS-E 910], carried into the manuals of [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].
- Substantiation by appropriate tests or other evidence [CS-E 910]: engine altitude testing or engine flight testing, or other tests or evidence the applicant proposes [AMC E 910(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 910|AMC E 910(2)]].
- Assessment of rapid relight after an in-flight shutdown and of rotor-lock [AMC E 910(3)], as part of the substantiation of [CS-E 910]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 910|AMC E 910(3)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies. For a multi-engine rotorcraft, the relight envelope is
what the crew uses after an in-flight shutdown. It is the counterpart at the
engine level to the OEI ratings: the OEI ratings keep the aircraft flying, and
the relight envelope defines when the failed engine can be brought back.

**The envelope is an engine deliverable consumed by the aircraft.**
[[AMC E 910|AMC E 910(1)]] recommends active coordination between the engine
type-certificate applicant and the aircraft type-certificate applicant. The
recommended envelope reaches the aircraft through the manuals for installing and
operating the engine [CS-E 20(d)].

**Starter assistance ties the envelope to the starting system.**
Whether the relight is assisted determines what the starting system must deliver
at altitude [CS-E 910]. Where starter assistance is required within the
envelope, the electrical or pneumatic supply becomes an installation assumption
under [[CS-E 30|CS-E 30(a)]].

**The Engine Control System is inside the obligation.**
Where the EECS-FADEC commands and schedules the relight, the relight capability
is part of the control system function assessed under [[CS-E 50]]. The fuel
schedule during relight is within the fuel system work of [[CS-E 560]].

[VERIFY: the maximum air speed and altitude for which relighting is recommended,
and whether starter assistance is required over any part of the envelope.
Neither is recorded in engine_profile.md. The air speed boundary depends on the
target rotorcraft, which is itself an open item.]
