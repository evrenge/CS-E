# CS-E 780 — Icing Conditions

> [!summary]
> The engine must function satisfactorily in flight and on the ground throughout
> the applicable atmospheric icing conditions, without unacceptable performance
> reduction, temperature increase, handling deterioration or mechanical damage.
> Which conditions are applicable comes from CS-Definitions and from the ice
> protection specifications of the aircraft code the engine is installed under.
> A separate sub-point covers ice ingestion, demonstrated by an ice slab
> ingestion test or by validated analysis.

## Requirement

### Functioning in icing conditions

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | Establish by tests, unless alternative appropriate evidence is available, that the engine will function satisfactorily in flight and on the ground when operated throughout the applicable atmospheric icing conditions, without unacceptable effects under (a)(1)(i) to (a)(1)(iv). | Required |
| **(a)(1)(i)** | …immediate or ultimate reduction of engine performance. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(ii)** | …increase of engine operating temperatures. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(iii)** | …deterioration of engine handling characteristics. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(iv)** | …mechanical damage. | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | The applicable atmospheric icing conditions must include the supercooled liquid water conditions defined in CS-Definitions Amendment 2 under 'Icing Atmospheric Conditions' for in-flight operation, freezing fog conditions for ground operation, and any additional conditions applicable to the engine air intake system in the ice protection specifications of the Certification Specifications applicable to the aircraft on which the engine is to be installed, as specified in CS-E 20(b). | Required |
| **(b)** | In showing compliance with CS-E 780(a), set all engine bleeds and mechanical power offtakes permitted during icing conditions at the level assumed to be the most critical, or simulate their effect by other acceptable means. | Required |
| **(b)** | Establish, however, that other likely use of bleed or mechanical power offtake will not lead to engine malfunctioning. | Required |
| **(c)** | In showing compliance with CS-E 780, take into account the conditions associated with a representative installation. | Required |
| **(d)** | If after the tests it is found that significant damage has occurred, further running or other evidence may be required to show that subsequent Failures are unlikely to occur. | Statement |
| **(e)** | Where an air intake guard is fitted, establish compliance with CS-E 780 with the guard in position, unless the guard is required to be retracted during icing conditions, in which case establish that its retraction is not affected immediately after a representative delay period. | Required |

The additional conditions named in (a)(2) are "ice crystal icing conditions,
supercooled large drop icing conditions, and snow conditions" [CS-E 780(a)(2)],
introduced as examples. Which of them apply is not decided by CS-E: it comes
from the ice protection specifications of the aircraft code, which
[[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(b)]] identifies.

Sub-point (b) sets two duties in opposite directions [CS-E 780(b)]. The test is
run at the **most critical** bleed and offtake setting, and separately it must
be established that **other likely** settings do not cause malfunction. Testing
the worst case alone does not satisfy the second.

Sub-point (e) treats a retractable guard as a hazard of its own. The question
becomes whether retraction still works after a representative delay in icing,
not whether the engine copes with the guard in place [CS-E 780(e)].

### Ice ingestion

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(f)(1)** | Demonstrate that the engine will function satisfactorily following the ingestion of defined quantities of ice, as part of compliance with CS-E 540. | Required |
| **(f)(1)** | Demonstrate compliance by an engine ice slab ingestion test, or by validated analysis showing equivalence to other means for demonstrating soft body damage tolerance. | Required |
| **(f)(2)** | Following the ingestion of ice under these conditions, the engine shall comply with CS-E 780(a). | Required |
| **(f)(3)** | For an engine that incorporates or requires the use of a protection device, compliance need not be demonstrated with respect to ice formed forward of the protection device, if (f)(3)(i), (ii) and (iii) are shown. | Relief |
| **(f)(3)(i)** | Such ice is of a size that will not pass through the protection device. | Required if claimed |
| **(f)(3)(ii)** | The protection device will withstand the impact of the ice. | Required if claimed |
| **(f)(3)(iii)** | The ice stopped by the protection device will not obstruct the flow of air into the engine, resulting in unacceptable effects under CS-E 780(a). | Required if claimed |
| **(f)(4)** | In establishing the ice slab ingestion conditions, determine the assumed ice quantity and dimensions, the ingestion velocity and the engine operating conditions, appropriate to the engine installation on the aircraft. | Required |
| **(f)(4)** | Include these assumptions in the manuals containing instructions for installing and operating the engine under CS-E 20(d). | Required |

Sub-point (f)(1) names two sources of ingested ice: ice released by the engine
air intake, including after delayed selection of the ice protection system, and
ice from other aircraft surfaces [CS-E 780(f)(1)]. The second is an installation
matter entirely.

The relief in (f)(3) has three cumulative conditions and covers only ice formed
**forward** of the protection device [CS-E 780(f)(3)]. It does not extend to ice
formed aft of the device.

Sub-point (f)(4) leaves the test severity to be determined rather than
prescribing it. It then requires the assumptions to be published in the
instructions for installing and operating the engine [CS-E 780(f)(4)], where the
installer finds them.

## Compliance

- Identification of the applicable atmospheric icing conditions, from CS-Definitions and from the ice protection specifications of the aircraft code identified under [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(b)]] [CS-E 780(a)(2)].
- Icing tests, or alternative appropriate evidence, covering in-flight and ground operation against the four unacceptable effects [CS-E 780(a)(1)].
- Bleed and mechanical power offtake settings at the most critical level, with evidence that other likely settings do not cause malfunction [CS-E 780(b)].
- Representative installation conditions taken into account [CS-E 780(c)].
- Further running or other evidence where significant damage is found [CS-E 780(d)].
- Air intake guard compliance, in position or with retraction established after a representative delay period [CS-E 780(e)].
- Ice slab ingestion test, or validated analysis showing equivalence to other soft body damage tolerance means [CS-E 780(f)(1)].
- Protection device evidence against the three conditions, where the (f)(3) relief is claimed [CS-E 780(f)(3)].
- Ice quantity, dimensions, ingestion velocity and engine operating conditions, published in the instructions for installing and operating the engine under [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]] [CS-E 780(f)(4)].
- Continuous ignition provisions where relied on for icing compliance [AMC E 720(a)]. Nothing in CS-E 780 refers to ignition; the link is made by the AMC to [[CS-E 720]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies in full. Nothing in it is restricted by engine type
[CS-E 780].

**The applicable conditions depend on the aircraft code.** Sub-point (a)(2)
names the ice protection specifications of CS-23, CS-25, CS-27 and CS-29
[CS-E 780(a)(2)]. For a rotorcraft the relevant ones are CS 27.1093(b) and
CS 29.1093(b), and which applies depends on the installation target. This is the
same open question recorded in [[CS-E 30]].

**The conditions are now readable, and the code choice does not change the
requirement.** Both documents behind sub-point (a)(2) are held.

CS-Definitions defines 'Icing Atmospheric Conditions' by two intensities,
Continuous Maximum Icing and Intermittent Maximum Icing, and gives the values in
Figures 2 to 7 [ext CS-Definitions, Icing Atmospheric Conditions]. Each is
"defined by the variables of the cloud liquid water content, the mean effective
diameter of the cloud droplets, the ambient air temperature, and the
inter-relationship of these three variables"
[ext CS-Definitions, Continuous Maximum Icing]. The values themselves are in
Figures 2 to 7 of CS-Definitions. The liquid water content is corrected for a
horizontal extent other than 17·4 n miles for continuous icing
[ext CS-Definitions, Continuous Maximum Icing] and 2.6 n miles for intermittent
icing [ext CS-Definitions, Intermittent Maximum Icing]. The figures are EASA's
and are not reproduced here; they are in CS-Definitions Amendment 2.

The additional conditions of the aircraft code resolve to three, and they are
the same in both rotorcraft codes: the flight power range under the icing
conditions of Appendix C, falling and blowing snow, and a 30-minute ground idle
with the icing protection air bleed at its critical condition, followed by
momentary operation at take-off power [ext CS 29.1093(b)],
[ext CS 27.1093(b)]. [[CS 29.1093]] and [[CS 27.1093]] set out each condition
in full, with its temperature band, liquid water content and drop diameter.

Neither code names an ice crystal condition or a supercooled large drop
condition in point 1093(b). CS-27 sends the applicant to "appendix C of CS-29"
for the icing conditions [ext CS 27.1093(b)(1)(i)], so the turbine engine
content of the two paragraphs is the same. **The open question of CS-27 against
CS-29 therefore does not change what CS-E 780(a) requires of this engine**,
although it stays open for other paragraphs — see [[CS-E 30]].

**Icing feeds back into the vibration survey.** [[AMC E 650|AMC E 650(5)]]
states that the conditions evaluated under [[CS-E 650|CS-E 650(a)]] should
include icing and rain and hail conditions. It also permits the CS-E 780 and
[[CS-E 790]] test evidence to be used to characterise the engine vibration
behaviour. Under [[AMC E 650|AMC E 650(9)(b)]], the stress margin criteria
should then account for those effects. The CS-E 780 results are therefore an
input to CS-E 650, not only to this paragraph.

**Ice ingestion is part of CS-E 540 compliance.** Sub-point (f)(1) says so
directly. [[AMC E 540|AMC E 540(2)]] confirms that CS-E 780 is among the
paragraphs intended to be sufficient for demonstrating
[[CS-E 540|CS-E 540(b)]] compliance for its own subject.

**Continuous ignition may be part of the answer.**
[[AMC E 720]] names ice ingestion and compliance with the icing specifications
among the reasons a continuously operated ignition system may be necessary. If
one is relied on here, [[CS-E 720]] binds.

**There is no accepted means for this engine, and the AMC says so itself.**
CS-E 780 points to "(See AMC E 780)", but that AMC excludes rotorcraft engines
in its own text: "Specific provisions for rotorcraft Engines are currently not
included in this AMC. Until guidance has been established, the necessary
compliance method required for rotorcraft Engines should be agreed by the
Agency." [AMC E 780(1.7)] It is therefore excluded from this vault and has no
note. The cross-reference is rendered as plain text above for that reason.

This is a different situation from the other paragraphs whose AMC is
aeroplane-only. Here the AMC exists, is detailed, and states that the rotorcraft
compliance method is a matter for agreement with the Agency rather than
something the AMC supplies. The compliance route for CS-E 780 is therefore
agreed with the Agency, not taken from the AMC.

## Amendment history

Amended at Amendment 7. Sub-point (a) was restructured and its definition of the
applicable conditions was replaced.

- **Before:** "It must be established by tests, unless alternative appropriate evidence is available, that the Engine will function satisfactorily when operated throughout the conditions of atmospheric icing (including freezing fog on ground) and falling and blowing snow defined in the turbine Engines air intake system ice protection specifications (CS-23.1093(b), CS-25.1093(b), CS-27.1093(b) or CS-29.1093(b)) of the Certification Specifications applicable to the aircraft on which the Engine is to be installed, as specified in CS-E 20(b) without unacceptable"
- **After:** the obligation split into (a)(1), which adds "in flight and on the ground" and refers to "the applicable atmospheric icing conditions", and a new (a)(2) which defines those conditions.

Three things changed in substance. The scope is now stated as "in flight and on
the ground" rather than left implicit. The conditions are sourced from
CS-Definitions Amendment 2 for in-flight supercooled liquid water and from
freezing fog for ground operation, where previously the whole definition sat in
the aircraft ice protection specifications. And the additional conditions were
widened: the old text named only falling and blowing snow, while the new text
names ice crystal icing conditions, supercooled large drop icing conditions and
snow conditions, still drawn from the aircraft code.

The CS-23 citation was also updated to distinguish CS 23.1093(b) until Amendment
4 from CS 23.2415 from Amendment 5. The four unacceptable effects were
re-lettered from (1) to (4) into (i) to (iv), and "and" became "and/or" before
the last one — so the effects are no longer read as cumulative.

The net effect is more work. Ice crystal and supercooled large drop conditions
were not named before, and the in-flight definition now comes from a document
the applicant must obtain separately.

The paragraph carries `[Amdt No: E/1]`, `[Amdt No: E/4]` and `[Amdt No: E/7]`.
