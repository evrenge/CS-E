# CS-E 720 — Continuous Ignition

> [!summary]
> This paragraph applies where approval is sought for an engine that permits or
> requires a continuously operated ignition system. Separate agreed tests must
> show the system safe and effective in the conditions where its use is permitted
> or required. Beyond that the applicant chooses one of two routes: run the
> system through a suitable engine endurance test representative of service
> usage, or run an equivalent rig programme and confirm it with at least 10 hours
> of engine endurance running.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Where approval of an engine is sought which permits or requires the use of a continuously-operated ignition system, meet the specifications of CS-E 720(b) together with either CS-E 720(c) or CS-E 720(d). | Required if claimed |
| **(b)** | Conduct separate tests, as agreed by the Agency, to show that continuous ignition systems are safe and effective in the conditions for which their use is permitted or required. | Required if claimed |
| **(c)** | Operate the system during a suitable engine endurance test for periods representative of the duration and frequency of operation of the system during likely service usage. | Required if claimed |
| **(c)** | That operation should be agreed by the Agency for individual cases. | Recommended |
| **(c)** | Generally, include in the schedule the use of continuous ignition for the maximum duration which is likely to be achieved in 1000 hours of service operation. | Required if claimed |
| **(d)** | It is acceptable to conduct an equivalent programme by appropriate rig testing, where this is possible. | Permitted |
| **(d)** | In that case, final confirmation of suitability of the equipment in the engine must be obtained by running at least 10 hours, in periods of at least ½ hour duration, of an engine endurance test with the ignition in operation at the appropriate engine conditions. | Required if claimed |

The structure is one mandatory element plus a choice. Sub-point (b) is required
in every case; (c) and (d) are alternatives, and the paragraph says so
explicitly: the specifications of CS-E 720(b) "together with either CS-E 720(c)
or (d) must be met" [CS-E 720(a)].

The rig route in (d) is not a way out of engine running. It reduces it to at
least 10 hours, in blocks of at least ½ hour, within an engine endurance
test.

The whole paragraph is conditional on seeking approval for continuous ignition.
The strength is Required if claimed for that reason: the system is elective, and
every obligation follows once it is sought.

The 1000-hour figure in (c) sets the exposure the schedule is scaled to, not a
test duration.

## Compliance

- Statement of whether approval is sought for a continuously operated ignition system, and of the conditions in which its use is permitted or required [CS-E 720(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 720]].
- Separate agreed tests showing the system safe and effective in those conditions [CS-E 720(b)].
- Either: an engine endurance test schedule with the system operated for periods representative of service usage, generally including the maximum duration likely in 1000 hours of service operation [CS-E 720(c)], within [[CS-E 740]].
- Or: an equivalent rig programme, plus at least 10 hours of engine endurance running with ignition in operation, in periods of at least ½ hour duration [CS-E 720(d)].
- Agency agreement on the test schedule for the individual case [CS-E 720(c)]. The source says this "should be agreed", so it is a recommendation inside a binding specification, not a condition of compliance.

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies if continuous ignition is sought. Nothing in it is
restricted by engine type or rating.

[[AMC E 720]] gives the reasons such a system may be needed: water or slush
ingestion at take-off, ice ingestion, or compliance with the icing
specifications. For this engine the icing route is the live one, and it links
CS-E 720 to [[CS-E 780]]. Where continuous ignition is relied on to meet the
icing specifications, the two paragraphs are substantiated together.

The ignition system itself is required by [[CS-E 500|CS-E 500(c)]], which
requires an igniter system suitable for starting on the ground and in flight up
to a declared altitude. CS-E 720 is about running that system continuously rather
than for starting, so the two are separate obligations on the same hardware.

[VERIFY: whether approval is sought for a continuously operated ignition system.
`engine_profile.md` does not record it. The answer decides whether CS-E 720 binds,
and whether the icing compliance of CS-E 780 depends on it.]

The relighting tests of [[CS-E 910]] and the starting tests of [[CS-E 750]] and
[[CS-E 770]] exercise the same ignition hardware under different conditions.
