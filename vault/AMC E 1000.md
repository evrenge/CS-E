# AMC E 1000 — Environmental and Operational Design Specifications - General

> [!summary]
> Two sentences explain why Subpart F exists. It defines engine design
> specifications needed to reach a configuration that a particular aircraft
> operation requires, and it defines what is needed at engine level to comply
> with CS-34. The subpart is therefore a bridge: the obligations originate with
> the aircraft operation or with CS-34, and Subpart F states the engine's part
> of them.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | CS-E Subpart F defines specifications for the design of an engine to determine a configuration which would be required as part of the approval for particular aircraft operations, such as ETOPS or Time Limited Dispatch. | Statement |
| — | It also defines the specifications necessary at the engine level for compliance with CS-34. | Statement |

AMC E 1000 is two unnumbered sentences, so the `Ref` cells carry no sub-point
identifier.

The AMC imposes no action. It explains the structure that [[CS-E 1000]] sets
out, and it identifies the two sources of obligation: a particular aircraft
operation, or CS-34.

The first sentence is why most of the subpart is elective. An engine does not
need a configuration for an aircraft operation nobody is seeking approval for,
so the applicant elects the paragraph when the operation is wanted.

## Compliance

- No compliance action arises from this AMC. It states the purpose of the subpart; the obligations sit in [[CS-E 1000]], [[CS-E 1010]], [[CS-E 1020]] and [[CS-E 1050]].

## Application to this engine

The AMC applies as a statement of scope.

The two aircraft operations it names as examples are ETOPS and time-limited
dispatch. Neither is in scope for this engine: `engine_profile.md` declares that
time-limited dispatch is not claimed, and ETOPS is an aeroplane operation
excluded from this vault by scope. The example is retained here because it
defines what kind of thing the subpart does: the operation drives the engine
configuration. That reasoning is what makes [[CS-E 1050]] elective too.

The CS-34 half of the AMC points to the part of Subpart F that may be
mandatory [CS-E 1000]. CS-34 defines no engine-level specification of its own:
CS 34.1 and CS 34.2 are one sentence each, and both refer the applicant to point
21.A.21 of Part 21 [ext CS 34.1], [ext CS 34.2]. What Subpart F states at engine
level therefore comes from the environmental protection requirements the Agency
designates under point 21.B.85 [ext GM1 34.1]. The chain ends at the Standards
and Recommended Practices (SARPs) of ICAO Annex 16.

CS-34 was repealed in May 2025, after CS-E Amendment 8 was issued. The
sentence in this AMC still names it. See [[CS 34.1]] and [[CS 34.2]] for the
chain and for what the repeal changed.
