# CS-E 160 — Tests - History

> [!summary]
> A part failure during certification testing cannot simply be repaired and the
> test resumed. Its cause must be determined, its effect on engine airworthiness
> assessed, and any necessary corrective actions determined and substantiated.
> The paragraph then reaches backwards: significant airworthiness-relevant events
> from the development programme that were not corrected before the certification
> tests must be assessed the same way.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Should a Failure of an engine part occur during the certification tests, determine its cause, in order to enable compliance with point 21.A.20(d)2 of Part 21. | Required |
| **(a)** | Assess the effect of that Failure on the airworthiness of the engine. | Required |
| **(a)** | Determine and substantiate any necessary corrective actions. | Required |
| **(b)** | Consider the development history of the engine, or of a component or equipment of the engine. | Required |
| **(b)** | Assess under CS-E 160(a) any significant event relevant to engine airworthiness that occurred during development and was not corrected before the certification tests. | Required |

Sub-point (b) is the part that is easy to underestimate. It extends (a) beyond
the certification tests themselves and into the development programme. The filter
has two conditions: the event must be significant and relevant to airworthiness,
and it must **not** have been corrected before certification testing began. An
event corrected before that point does not have to be assessed under (a). The
correction itself is what removes that duty, so the development history still
has to be examined to establish it.

The duty in (a) is threefold and the three parts are separate: cause, effect, and
corrective action. Determining a cause without assessing the airworthiness effect
does not satisfy (a), and a corrective action must be substantiated rather than
merely stated.

## Compliance

- Failure investigation record for each part Failure during certification testing, giving the determined cause [CS-E 160(a)].
- Airworthiness effect assessment for each such Failure [CS-E 160(a)].
- Corrective action definition and substantiation, where corrective action is necessary [CS-E 160(a)].
- Development history review of the engine and of its components and equipment, identifying significant airworthiness-relevant events [CS-E 160(b)].
- Assessment under (a) of each such event not corrected before the certification tests [CS-E 160(b)].

## Application to this engine

Both sub-points apply; neither is restricted by engine type, rating or control
system.

CS-E 160 has no AMC of its own. It governs every test in the certification
programme. So it reaches all the Subpart E testing: the calibration test of
[[CS-E 730]], the endurance tests of [[CS-E 740]], and the rest. The test
conditions that those tests run under are set by [[CS-E 140]] and [[CS-E 150]];
CS-E 160 governs what happens when one of them produces a part Failure.

For this engine, the OEI ratings widen the population of tests that (b) reaches.
The 30-Second OEI and 2-Minute OEI ratings are substantiated by dedicated test
sequences. So development events affecting the parts exercised at those ratings
fall under (b), alongside events from all-engines-operating running.

**What point 21.A.20(d)2 asks for.** Part 21 is now held. After completing all
compliance demonstrations, including the inspections and tests of point 21.A.33
and the flight tests of point 21.A.35, the applicant must declare that "no
feature or characteristic has been identified that may make the product, the UAS
or the CMU unsafe for the uses for which certification is requested"
[ext 21.A.20(d)2].

The guidance explains why such a declaration is asked for at all: "while every
effort is made to address in the applicable certification basis all the risks to
product safety that may be caused by the product, experience shows that
safety-related events may occur with products in service, even though compliance
with the certification basis is fully demonstrated" [ext GM 21.A.20(d)].

That is the reason CS-E 160 exists in the form it takes. An engine part Failure
during a certification test is a candidate feature or characteristic of exactly
that kind. Sub-point (a) makes the applicant determine its cause and assess its
effect on airworthiness before the declaration can honestly be made, and (b)
extends the same treatment to development testing. The Part 21 reference gives
the reason for the duty; the duty itself is stated in full in CS-E 160(a).

## Amendment history

Amended at Amendment 7. The cited Part 21 provision was replaced:

- **Before:** "In order to enable compliance with 21.A.21(c)(3) of Part 21, should a Failure of an Engine part occur during the certification tests"
- **After:** "In order to enable compliance with point 21.A.20(d)2 of Part 21, should a Failure of an Engine part occur during the certification tests"

Amendment 7 deleted "21.A.21(c)(3)" and inserted "point" and "21.A.20(d)2". This
is a different provision, not only a citation style correction, which sets it
apart from the change made to [[CS-E 120|CS-E 120(a)]] at the same amendment. The
CS-E duty itself — determine the cause, assess the airworthiness effect,
substantiate corrective action — is unchanged in wording and in strength.

The current point 21.A.20(d)2 is recorded above. The former point 21.A.21(c)(3)
cannot be read from the edition held: the Part 21 consolidation in
`source/external/` is dated November 2025 and carries the numbering after the
change, where point 21.A.21 sets the conditions for issuing a type certificate
and the declaration duty sits in point 21.A.20(d) [ext 21.A.21(a)].
