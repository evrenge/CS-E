# CS-E 640 — Pressure Loads

> [!summary]
> Every static part carrying significant gas or liquid pressure must survive two
> pressure levels for a stabilised minute. At the lower level it must show no
> permanent distortion beyond serviceable limits and no leakage that could cause
> a Hazardous Engine Effect. At the higher level it must not fracture or burst.
> Each level is the greatest of three candidate pressures. The demonstration must
> account for part temperature, other static loads, minimum material and process
> properties, and adverse geometry.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Establish by test, validated analysis or a combination thereof that all static parts subject to significant gas or liquid pressure loads meet (a)(1) and (a)(2) for a stabilised period of one minute. | Required |
| **(a)(1)** | Exhibit no permanent distortion beyond serviceable limits, and no leakage which could result in a Hazardous Engine Effect, when subjected to the greater of 1.1 times the maximum working pressure, 1.33 times the normal working pressure, or 35 kPa above the normal working pressure. | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Exhibit no fracture or burst when subjected to the greater of 1·15 times the maximum possible pressure, 1·5 times the maximum working pressure, or 35 kPa above the maximum possible pressure. | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | Take account of the operating temperature of the part in showing compliance with CS-E 640(a). | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | Take account of any other significant static loads in addition to pressure loads. | Required |
| **(b)(3)** | Take account of minimum properties representative of both the material and the processes used in the construction of the part. | Required |
| **(b)(4)** | Take account of any adverse geometry conditions allowed by the type design. | Required |

Each level is the **greatest** of its three candidates, not a choice among them.
The three are evaluated and the largest governs, so a part with a low working
pressure is still tested to at least 35 kPa above it.

The two levels rest on different reference pressures, and the distinction is
decisive. The distortion and leakage level (a)(1) is built on the **normal** and
**maximum working** pressures. The fracture and burst level (a)(2) is built on
the **maximum working** and **maximum possible** pressures, and maximum possible
includes Failure conditions. [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(1)]] defines all three terms.

Sub-point (b)(3) requires **minimum** properties of both material and process. A
demonstration against typical or measured properties does not satisfy it, which
is the same discipline [[CS-E 70|CS-E 70(a)]] imposes on design values.

"For a stabilised period of one minute" applies to both levels.

## Compliance

- Identification of the static parts subject to significant gas or liquid pressure loads [CS-E 640(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(1)]].
- Determination of the normal working, maximum working and maximum possible pressures for each such part [CS-E 640(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(1)]].
- Proof pressure evidence at the (a)(1) level against permanent distortion beyond serviceable limits and against leakage causing a Hazardous Engine Effect [CS-E 640(a)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(2)]]; this is the proof pressure item of [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(c)]].
- Burst pressure evidence at the (a)(2) level [CS-E 640(a)(2)]. This is the burst pressure item of [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(c)]].
- Serviceable limits from the anticipated engine manual, used as the distortion acceptance criteria [CS-E 640(a)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(2)]].
- Analysis of additional loads and their effect, with the test carried out under those loads where they are significant [CS-E 640(b)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(3)]].
- Test temperature justification, or an increased test pressure differential simulating the loss of properties with temperature [CS-E 640(b)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(3)]].
- Minimum material and process properties used in the substantiation [CS-E 640(b)(3)], derived under [[CS-E 70|CS-E 70(a)]].
- Adverse geometry conditions allowed by the type design, accounted for [CS-E 640(b)(4)].
- Where an analytical model is used, its validation by testing or successful field experience with parts of similar design [CS-E 640(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(4)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies in full; no sub-point is restricted by engine type.

The OEI ratings reach this paragraph through the pressure definitions.
[[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(1)]] names "use of OEI ratings" among the adverse
operational conditions that set both the maximum working pressure and the
maximum possible pressure. This engine declares 30-Second OEI, 2-Minute OEI and
Continuous OEI, so those ratings raise the pressures that (a)(1) and (a)(2) are
computed from, and therefore raise the test levels themselves.

CS-E 640 shares its test method with the static pressure loaded part lifing of
[[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(3)(e)]]: the additional-loads treatment, the temperature
substitution and the mounting and restraint conditions are the same text in both
places. The two paragraphs ask different questions of the same test — CS-E 640
asks whether the part survives the pressure, CS-E 515 asks how long it lasts.

Casings are the principal population. [[CS-E 520|CS-E 520(d)]] requires design
consideration against major rupture of engine casings following a local Failure,
and [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(d)]] makes a casing whose ultimate crack growth could
cause a Hazardous Engine Effect a Critical Part under [[CS-E 515]].
