# AMC E 930 — Initial Maintenance Programme Test

> [!summary]
> New at Amendment 8, and the longest AMC in Subpart E. It defines four terms,
> states the purpose of the IMP test, says what happens after a design change,
> and then sets out the test itself: cycle assessment, engine configuration, test
> parameters, duration, pass/fail criteria, and what credit the result carries
> into the maintenance programme. A final part offers a fixed engine overhaul
> period as a simplified IMP.

## Requirement

### AMC E 930(a) — Definitions

| Ref | Term | Definition | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | Engine flight cycle | Predicted average flight profile of the engine parameters and conditions representative of the way the engine is expected to operate in service. | Statement |
| **(a)(2)** | Initial maintenance programme (IMP) | Instructions for continued airworthiness submitted under CS-E 25 that are considered necessary to ensure sufficient engine reliability. The ICA may be required by the type certificate holder in the Airworthiness Limitations section, or may be recommended at certain intervals. The IMP may use hard time-based maintenance programming, on-condition-based maintenance programming, or a combination of both. | Statement |
| **(a)(3)** | Initial maintenance inspection (IMI) intervals | An IMP approach for maintenance tasks based on hard time policy, that is, maximum hours or cycles that an engine or engine module should be operated before a maintenance task is performed. | Statement |
| **(a)(4)** | Engine overhaul | The process to disassemble, clean, inspect, repair or replace as necessary, reassemble and test for return-to-service approval within the manufacturer's overhaul data specifications. It relates to the periodic disassembly of the entire engine, or modules when applicable, rather than maintenance of individual parts or assemblies. | Statement |

The Engine flight cycle is the pivot of the whole AMC: it is
"Predicted average flight profile of the Engine parameters and conditions
representative of the way the Engine is expected to operate in service."
[AMC E 930(a)(1)] Every later judgement — cycle representativeness, duration,
credit — is made against it.

### AMC E 930(b) — Purpose of the IMP test

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(b)** | The primary purpose of the IMP test run is to demonstrate sufficient engine reliability and support the establishment of the entry-into-service (EIS) IMP for that type design. | Statement |
| **(b)(1)–(3)** | The AMC accordingly provides guidance and acceptable means of compliance on test methods and procedures, test pass/fail criteria, and EIS IMP requirements or recommendations. | Statement |

### AMC E 930(c) — Applicability to changes to type certificate

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(c)** | Following the initial demonstration of compliance with CS-E 930, Minor design changes do not require a new demonstration of compliance. | Relief |
| **(c)** | For Major design changes, assess the impact on the validity of the previously established IMP. | Accepted method |
| **(c)** | Either substantiate the previous IMP or propose means to substantiate a change to it. Such means may include analysis, testing or a combination of both. | Accepted method |
| **(c)** | A full test to redemonstrate compliance with CS-E 930 may not be required. | Statement |
| **(c)** | When assessing the IMP's validity, evaluate the impact of the change on the engine design factors and engine system dynamics. | Accepted method |

The AMC then lists what it means by each term.

**Engine design factors**, to the extent that they contribute to the system
dynamics of the engine: mechanical size and stiffness, static structure, dynamics
of engine, bearing arrangement, number of structural frames, arrangement of
mounting to aircraft, number of compression and turbine stages, changes to engine
control system architecture such as supervisory to full authority type or
analogue to digital, changes to installation such as service bleed and power
extraction, and new technology in design, materials or manufacturing processes.
The list is not closed [AMC E 930(c)].

**System dynamics of the engine** are the vibratory response signature, meaning
system critical frequencies and amplitudes. These can be a function of engine
mass and stiffness, rotor speeds, and engine or aircraft mount and strut mass and
stiffness [AMC E 930(c)].

### AMC E 930(d)(1) — IMP test cycle assessment

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)(1)(i)** | Provide an assessment of expected service operating conditions as part of the test plan, showing that the proposed test cycle represents the expected in-service engine flight cycles. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(1)(i)** | The assessment covers established power ratings, component stress and temperature, exhaust gas temperature (EGT), engine vibration, cycle / operating time cumulative damage, and other critical factors. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(1)(i)** | Where the engine includes OEI power ratings, include representation of the cumulative usage of those OEI power ratings before maintenance in the engine flight cycles of the IMP test. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(1)(i)** | For multiple aircraft applications, show that the test cycle adequately represents all identified or anticipated installations and engine flight cycles. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(1)(i)** | Test cycles used in the past are the full cycle, the accelerated severity cycle, and combinations of the two. | Statement |
| **(d)(1)(ii)** | A full cycle test runs the engine through the exact power setting sequences for the time periods identified in the engine flight cycle. One complete cycle includes a typical engine flight cycle and the exact number of operating hours, from engine start through complete shutdown. | Statement |
| **(d)(1)(iii)** | An accelerated severity cycle test provides a rigorous test of engines or engine parts for which durability is primarily affected by cyclic operation. It allows the time at various power settings and the sequence of power selections to be varied. | Statement |
| **(d)(1)(iii)** | To relate the accelerated severity cycle test to the full flight cycle, perform a detailed analysis of the stress, the temperature, and the resulting life of each affected part of the engine. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(1)(iii)** | The accelerated severity cycle test may include the equivalent of several flight cycles during a given portion of the overall engine test, which can give a small number of engine hours against the number of engine flight cycles demonstrated. | Statement |
| **(d)(1)(iii)** | The accelerated severity cycle test is generally not considered ideal for engine parts whose durability is primarily affected by hours of operation rather than by cycles. For those cases, the IMP substantiation may require other test or in-service experience data. | Statement |
| **(d)(1)(iii)** | The accelerated severity cycle test should include engine start and shutdown. | Accepted method |

The two cycle types answer different questions. A full cycle test buys hours and
cycles at face value. An accelerated severity cycle test buys cycles cheaply but
leaves the time-dependent damage mechanisms — creep, oxidation, wear at load —
undersubstantiated, and the AMC says so directly.

### AMC E 930(d)(2) — IMP test engine configuration

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)(2)(i)** | CS-E 930 requires the test to be performed with an engine that adequately conforms to its final type design, so no significant engine modification should be required to complete the IMP test. | Statement |
| **(d)(2)(i)** | Engine parts included in the type design but not installed on the test engine may be substantiated by taking credit for validated analyses and/or other tests. Equipment qualification performed under CS-E 80(b) may be considered an appropriate means of compliance in this case. | Permitted |
| **(d)(2)(ii)** | Include in the IMP test other hardware items, or representative hardware items, that are not normally part of the engine type design — for example an air starter or engine build-up hardware — and that have an impact on the reliability of the engine. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(2)(iii)** | Perform the IMP test with the engine installed in a typical configuration, to the maximum extent possible. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(2)(iii)** | Connect and operate, in a way representative of the intended service, the airframe accessories and the interfaces that load the engine. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(2)(iii)** | Schedule, throughout the test, typical accessory loads and bleed air extraction that would be experienced during the engine flight cycle. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(2)(v)** | For turboshaft applications, load the test engine output shaft to simulate the appropriate rotor drive system characteristics of the intended installation. Potential characteristics include but are not limited to inertial and torsional vibration. | Accepted method |

Sub-point (d)(2)(v) is the turboshaft provision and it is short: "For turboshaft
applications, the applicant should load the test Engine output shaft to simulate
the appropriate rotor drive system characteristics of the intended installation."
[AMC E 930(d)(2)(v)] The two characteristics named, inertia and torsional
vibration, are the ones that determine what the engine actually experiences on a
rotorcraft.

### AMC E 930(d)(3) and (d)(4) — Test parameters and duration

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)(3)** | The conditions achieved during the test should effectively represent the conditions expected during an engine flight cycle, including power, stress, component temperature, EGT and unbalance vibration. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(4)** | The total number of test cycles and the test duration should be sufficient to demonstrate the effectiveness of the IMP. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(4)** | CS-E 930 does not require a fixed number of cycles. The applicant should correlate the number of cycles proposed for the test to the planned engine IMP. | Statement |

The Note under (d)(4) forestalls the obvious misreading: "CS-E 930 does not
require a fixed number of cycles (i.e. the IMP test is not a 1 000-cycle test)."
[AMC E 930(d)(4)] The cycle count is set by the maintenance interval being
claimed, not by a figure in the rule.

### AMC E 930(d)(5) — Pass/fail criteria

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)(5)(i)** | The engine type design will comply with CS-E 930 when the post-test hardware condition demonstrates that the engine will remain airworthy when applying the proposed IMP. The engine should comply with (d)(5)(ii), (iii) and (iv). | Statement |
| **(d)(5)(ii)** | Over the test duration, and when following normal ICA maintenance practices, the engine should meet all proposed power ratings without exceeding any operating limitations. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(5)(ii)** | The engine should be free of significant anomalies, such as surge or stall, when operated as per the operating instructions provided in compliance with CS-E 20(d). | Accepted method |
| **(d)(5)(iii)** | A post-test teardown inspection should demonstrate that each engine part conforms to the type design; or that damage is evaluated and the part is considered acceptable for safe continued operation; or that damage is evaluated and the part is considered not acceptable for safe continued operation, but there is no imminent failure identified at the end of test. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(5)(iii)** | Engine parts may be considered acceptable for safe continued operation if the applicant includes appropriate inspections and/or limitations within the ICA. | Permitted |
| **(d)(5)(iii)** | For parts damaged beyond criteria ensuring safe continued operation, substantiate the finding that there is no imminent failure and provide in the ICA an appropriate fixed engine overhaul period. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(5)(iii)** | For engines having 30-second OEI and 2-minute OEI power ratings, the IMP test results may be used when showing compliance with CS-E 25(b)(2) for OEI power availability demonstration at the end of the fixed engine overhaul period. | Permitted |
| **(d)(5)(iv)** | The certification documentation should identify those parts of the engine that will have specific ICA requirements or recommendations resulting from the IMP test. | Accepted method |
| **(d)(5)(iv)** | The final ICA should include these specific requirements and/or recommendations, in compliance with CS-E 25, including life limits, inspections, intervals, and accept/reject criteria. | Accepted method |

The teardown criterion is a three-way test, not a pass or fail. Conformity is one
outcome; acceptable damage with ICA inspections or limitations is a second; and
unacceptable damage with no imminent failure is a third, which is survivable but
costs a fixed engine overhaul period in the ICA.

### AMC E 930(d)(6) — Determination of the IMP

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)(6)(i)** | For a successful full cycle test, the applicant may take credit for the full number of cycles and the full number of hours demonstrated during the test when proposing an IMP. | Permitted |
| **(d)(6)(ii)** | For a successful accelerated severity cycle test, the applicant may take credit for the full number of cycles for those engine parts for which the test cycle was shown to be equal to or more severe than the assumed engine flight cycle. | Permitted |
| **(d)(6)(iii)** | Where the test cycle involves a high power setting operation for durations that significantly exceed those of the engine flight cycle, EASA may accept that the applicant takes credit for intervals longer than the IMP test length. | Permitted |
| **(d)(6)(iii)** | This approach requires caution, because some engine parts will wear as a function of time at load rather than from low cycle fatigue, and life extrapolation based on material property data alone is imprecise. | Statement |
| **(d)(6)(iii)** | Under these circumstances, the applicant may need to draw supporting evidence from other engine tests, component tests or sub-assembly tests. | Accepted method |

The credit rules mirror the cycle types. A full cycle test earns hours and cycles;
an accelerated severity cycle test earns cycles only, and only for the parts the
severity argument covers.

### AMC E 930(e) — Fixed engine overhaul period

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(e)** | A simplified IMP may consist of a fixed engine overhaul period. | Permitted |
| **(e)** | If this approach is selected, perform the engine test of CS-E 930 described in paragraph (d), and substantiate the fixed engine overhaul period from the test results as per paragraph (d)(5)(iii). | Accepted method |

Part (e) simplifies the programme, not the test. The test of (d) is run either
way.

## Compliance

### Test planning

- An Engine flight cycle definition, being the predicted average flight profile for the intended service [AMC E 930(a)(1)].
- A test plan containing the assessment of expected service operating conditions and the cycle representativeness argument [AMC E 930(d)(1)(i)].
- A statement of the cycle type selected: full cycle, accelerated severity cycle, or a combination [AMC E 930(d)(1)(i)].
- Where an accelerated severity cycle is used, a detailed stress, temperature and life analysis relating it to the full flight cycle [AMC E 930(d)(1)(iii)].
- Where multiple aircraft applications are sought, evidence that the cycle covers all identified or anticipated installations [AMC E 930(d)(1)(i)].
- Representation of cumulative OEI rating usage before maintenance within the test cycles [AMC E 930(d)(1)(i)].
- A cycle count correlated to the planned IMP intervals [AMC E 930(d)(4)].

### Test build and conduct

- A conformity statement for the test engine against the final type design [AMC E 930(d)(2)(i)].
- Substantiation by validated analysis or other tests for type-design parts not installed, which may draw on equipment qualification under [[CS-E 80|CS-E 80(b)]] [AMC E 930(d)(2)(i)].
- Inclusion of reliability-relevant hardware outside the type design, or representative items [AMC E 930(d)(2)(ii)].
- A typical installed configuration with airframe accessories and loading interfaces connected and operated representatively [AMC E 930(d)(2)(iii)].
- A scheduled accessory load and bleed extraction profile through the test [AMC E 930(d)(2)(iii)].
- Output shaft loading representing the rotor drive system, including inertia and torsional vibration [AMC E 930(d)(2)(v)].
- Recorded test parameters covering power, stress, component temperature, EGT and unbalance vibration [AMC E 930(d)(3)].

### Evidence and outcome

- Test records showing all proposed power ratings met without exceeding operating limitations, and no significant anomalies [AMC E 930(d)(5)(ii)].
- A post-test teardown inspection report resolving every part into one of the three outcomes of [AMC E 930(d)(5)(iii)].
- Where damage is beyond safe continued operation criteria: a no-imminent-failure substantiation and a fixed engine overhaul period in the ICA [AMC E 930(d)(5)(iii)].
- Certification documentation identifying the parts carrying specific ICA requirements or recommendations from the test [AMC E 930(d)(5)(iv)].
- The final ICA content under [[CS-E 25]] — life limits, inspections, intervals, accept/reject criteria [AMC E 930(d)(5)(iv)].
- The IMP credit claim, stating which parts take cycle credit and which take hours credit [AMC E 930(d)(6)].
- Where intervals longer than the test length are claimed: supporting evidence from other engine, component or sub-assembly tests [AMC E 930(d)(6)(iii)].
- Where the IMP is a fixed engine overhaul period: its substantiation from the teardown results [AMC E 930(e)].

### After a design change

- For a Major design change: an assessment of the impact on the previously established IMP, covering engine design factors and engine system dynamics, with either a substantiation of the existing IMP or proposed means to substantiate a change [AMC E 930(c)].

## Application to this engine

The AMC applies, and one of its sub-points is written for this engine type.

**AMC E 930(d)(2)(v) makes the test rig an installation-dependent item.**
The output shaft must be loaded to simulate the rotor drive system of the
intended installation, with inertia and torsional vibration named. A
water-brake or eddy-current dynamometer without a representative inertia and
torsional response does not meet the sub-point on its own. The rig specification
therefore depends on the target rotorcraft transmission, which
`engine_profile.md` records as an open item.

**OEI usage has to be assumed before the test, not measured after it.**
[AMC E 930(d)(1)(i)] requires the cumulative usage of the OEI ratings before
maintenance to be represented in the test cycles. That is a service-usage
assumption about how often a multi-engine rotorcraft operator will actually reach
30-Second, 2-Minute or Continuous OEI power. It is the same assumption that
drives the usage recording provisions of [[CS-E 60|CS-E 60(d)]] and the
post-flight inspection regime of [[CS-E 25|CS-E 25(b)(2)]].

**The CS-E 25(b)(2) credit is worth designing the test around.**
[AMC E 930(d)(5)(iii)] permits the IMP test results to be used in showing
compliance with [[CS-E 25|CS-E 25(b)(2)]] for OEI power availability at the end
of the fixed engine overhaul period. [[CS-E 25|CS-E 25(b)(2)]] otherwise requires
an in-service engine evaluation programme with service engine tests or equivalent
experience. Taking the credit means the OEI power availability demonstration must
be planned into the IMP test cycle and its post-test power calibration.

**Accessory loads and bleed are part of the cycle, not a fixed setting.**
[AMC E 930(d)(2)(iii)] requires typical accessory loads and bleed air extraction
to be scheduled throughout the test. On a rotorcraft that includes the
environmental control and anti-icing bleed demands, which are installation
assumptions declared under [[CS-E 30]].

**The control system architecture is a listed design factor.**
[AMC E 930(c)] names "changes to Engine control system architecture (e.g.
supervisory to full authority type, or analogue to digital)" among the engine
design factors to evaluate after a Major change. A change to the EECS-FADEC
architecture therefore reopens the IMP validity assessment as well as the
[[CS-E 50]] substantiation.

[VERIFY: the rotor drive system inertia and torsional characteristics required to
specify the IMP test load, per AMC E 930(d)(2)(v). These come from the aircraft
transmission and are not an engine-side quantity.]

[VERIFY: whether the CS-E 25(b)(2) credit of AMC E 930(d)(5)(iii) is claimed. It
requires a fixed engine overhaul period to exist, so it is only available where
the IMP is the simplified form of AMC E 930(e) or where a fixed overhaul period
arises from the teardown findings.]

[VERIFY: the assumed cumulative OEI rating usage between maintenance events, for
the test cycle of AMC E 930(d)(1)(i).]

## Not applicable

- **(d)(1)(i)** — "reverse thrust use" in the cycle assessment list. Thrust reversers are aeroplane equipment.
- **(d)(2)(ii)** — the thrust reverser example among hardware outside the type design. The air starter and engine build-up hardware examples are kept.
- **(d)(2)(iv)** — Turbopropeller Applications in full, including propeller braking and APU-mode operations. Propeller provisions are outside scope.
- **(c)** — "size and diameter of the fan" among the engine design factors. This engine has no fan.
- **(d)(1)(i)**, **(d)(7)** — the AMC 20-6B early ETOPS route in full: the option to combine the IMP test with the early ETOPS test, the four conditions of (d)(7)(i), and the seven on-wing inspections of (d)(7)(ii). ETOPS is an aeroplane operation, excluded by the scope rule, and CS-E 1040 is excluded with it. The alternative is therefore unavailable and the IMP test of (d) is run on its own.
- Throughout — "thrust or power" is rendered as power. The engine produces shaft power; the thrust half of each pair has no turboshaft case.

## Amendment history

AMC E 930 is **new at Amendment 8**. The Change Information declares it as "The
following AMC E 930 is added:" and records 260 inserted runs against no deletions,
because no AMC E 930 existed in Amendment 7. [work/redline/AMC_E_930_Amdt8.md]

The whole of the guidance above is new. There is no prior wording to compare, and
no obligation was relaxed: the AMC arrived with the specification it serves.
