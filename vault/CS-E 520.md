# CS-E 520 — Strength

> [!summary]
> Four specifications govern the strength of a turbine engine. Major rotating
> components must withstand normal thermal and dynamic conditions and the
> excessive conditions that abnormal speeds, temperatures or vibration loads can
> produce. Fixed structure near rotating parts must be arranged so that any rub
> does not cause a Hazardous Engine Effect, or a warning device must be provided.
> Blade shedding must be radially contained, and validated data on out-of-balance
> forces must be given to the aircraft constructor. Casing design must avoid
> major rupture following a local failure.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Give the major rotating components of the engine adequate strength to withstand both the thermal and dynamic conditions of normal operation, and any excessive thermal or dynamic conditions that may result from abnormal speeds, abnormal temperatures or abnormal vibration loads. | Required |
| **(a)** | In assessing the abnormal conditions to be considered, take account of the Failure analysis prescribed in CS-E 510. | Required |
| **(b)** | Arrange fixed structure in close proximity to rotating parts so that any rub caused by (b)(1) or (b)(2) will occur in a manner not likely to result in a Hazardous Engine Effect; as an alternative, provide a device giving warning of such unintended movement. | Required |
| **(b)(1)** | The rub causes to be covered include thermal expansion or contraction of parts to the extremes of movement within the operating envelope of the engine. | Required |
| **(b)(2)** | …and movement resulting from likely Fault conditions of either the fixed or rotating parts. | Required |
| **(c)(1)** | Design the engine so that debris resulting from the shedding of compressor or turbine blades, either singly or in likely combinations, will be radially contained. | Required |
| **(c)(2)** | Establish and provide validated data, from analysis or test or both, enabling each aircraft constructor to ascertain the forces that could be imposed on the aircraft structure and systems as a consequence of out-of-balance running, and during any continued rotation with rotor unbalance after shutdown of the engine following blade Failure as demonstrated in compliance with CS-E 810. | Required |
| **(c)(2)** | Where the Failure of a shaft, bearing or bearing support, or a bird strike event as required under CS-E 800, results in higher forces being developed, consider those Failures also — except for bird strike in relation to continued out-of-balance running. | Required |
| **(d)** | Give design consideration to avoiding the risk of major rupture of engine casings, particularly those subjected to high pressure loads, in the event of a local Failure in the casing or damage to the casing arising, for example, from a torching flame following a combustion system Failure. | Required |

Sub-point (b) offers a genuine alternative, and the two branches are not
equivalent in effort. Either the rub is shown to occur in a manner not likely to
cause a Hazardous Engine Effect, or a warning device for the unintended movement
is provided.

Sub-point (c)(1) requires **radial** containment specifically. Forward and
rearward release is not covered by it, and
[[AMC E 510|AMC E 510(3)(d)(iii)]] is explicit that blade debris released
forward, rearward or otherwise outside the containment structure still needs its
own probability assessment against [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(3)]].

The bird strike carve-out in (c)(2) is narrow and easy to misread. Bird strike
forces are considered, but not "in relation to continued out-of-balance running"
[CS-E 520(c)(2)].

Sub-point (a) is not self-contained: the abnormal conditions to be assessed are
an output of the safety analysis of [[CS-E 510]], not a separate judgement.

## Compliance

- Strength substantiation for major rotating components across normal and abnormal thermal and dynamic conditions, with the abnormal conditions traced to the Failure analysis of [[CS-E 510]] [CS-E 520(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(a)]].
- Rub analysis for fixed structure near rotating parts, covering thermal expansion and contraction across the operating envelope and movement from likely Fault conditions; or the warning device and its substantiation [CS-E 520(b)].
- Radial containment demonstration for compressor and turbine blade shedding, singly and in likely combinations [CS-E 520(c)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(c)(1)]]. The blade loss test itself is [[CS-E 810]].
- Declared containment angle limits, made available to the aircraft constructor [CS-E 520(c)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(c)(1)]].
- Validated engine model and out-of-balance force data provided to each aircraft constructor, covering out-of-balance running and continued rotation with rotor unbalance after shutdown [CS-E 520(c)(2)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(c)(2)]]. The installation assumptions are documented under [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].
- Assessment of shaft, bearing and bearing support Failure, and of bird strike under [[CS-E 800]], where these produce higher forces [CS-E 520(c)(2)].
- Casing design assessment against major rupture following local failure or torching flame damage [CS-E 520(d)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(d)]].

## Application to this engine

All four sub-points apply; none is restricted by engine type or rating.

Sub-point (a) reaches the declared ratings. The dynamic conditions of normal
operation include operation at 30-Second OEI, 2-Minute OEI and Continuous OEI, so
the rotating component strength case covers those speeds and temperatures as well
as the all-engines-operating ratings. The Subpart A strength specification in
[[CS-E 100]] applies in parallel, and the two must be read together.

Sub-point (c)(2) is an installation deliverable rather than an internal analysis.
The output goes to the aircraft constructor, which for this engine means the
rotorcraft manufacturer, and the interface assumptions are recorded in the
manuals of [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]]. [[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(c)(2)]] names "dynamic
displacement of interface features between Engine and aircraft" as part of the
validated data, an item Amendment 7 added.

Sub-point (d) connects to [[CS-E 130]]: the torching flame following a combustion
system Failure is a fire case, and a casing whose ultimate Failure could cause a
Hazardous Engine Effect becomes a Critical Part under
[[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(2)]] and [[CS-E 515]], as
[[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(d)]] states.

[[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(c)(2)]] refers to adding imbalances to "the fan and other
rotors" when verifying the engine model. A turboshaft has no fan; the compressor
and turbine rotors carry the equivalent verification, cross-referenced to
[[CS-E 650]].

## Amendment history

Amended at Amendment 7. Sub-point (c)(1) was rewritten, and the change replaced a
consequence-based test with a design-based one:

- **Before:** "The strength of the Engine must be such that the shedding of compressor or turbine blades, either singly or in likely combinations, will not result in a Hazardous Engine Effect (e.g. as a long term effect in respect of those Failures which would not be detected by the declared instrumentation, such as vibration detectors) and within the likely shutdown time for those which would be detected, and during any continued rotation after shutdown."
- **After:** "The design of the Engine must be such that debris resulting from the shedding of compressor or turbine blades, either singly or in likely combinations, will be radially contained"

The old wording asked whether blade shedding results in a Hazardous Engine
Effect, across three time windows: as a long-term effect for undetected Failures,
within the likely shutdown time for detected ones, and during continued rotation
after shutdown. The new wording asks a direct design question — is the debris
radially contained. It also moves the subject from "strength" to "design".

The obligation is not simply narrower. The consequence question the old text
asked has not disappeared; it moved to [[CS-E 510]], where
[[AMC E 510|AMC E 510(3)(d)(iii)]] — rewritten at the same amendment — requires
the probability of an unsafe condition from non-radial blade debris to be
assessed explicitly. Read together, the two amendments separate a containment
duty in CS-E 520 from a probability duty in CS-E 510.

[[AMC E 520|AMC E 520(c)(2)]] was also amended at Amendment 7, adding the dynamic
displacement item to the validated data list, and adding the requirement to
evaluate the most severe blade Failure that would not cause Failure of the rotor
structural support.

The paragraph carries `[Amdt. No.: E/1]`, `[Amdt. No.: E/2]` and
`[Amdt. No.: E/7]`.
