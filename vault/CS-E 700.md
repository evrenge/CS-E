# CS-E 700 — Excess Operating Conditions

> [!summary]
> Where any operating condition substantiated elsewhere in Subpart E could be
> exceeded in normal or likely emergency conditions within the declared flight
> envelope, the most severe conditions likely to occur must be shown to have no
> unacceptable effects on the engine. The paragraph is a catch-all: it does not
> add a test, it requires the margins established by other tests to be checked
> against what the envelope can actually produce.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Where any of the operating conditions substantiated elsewhere in this subpart — for example air or gas pressure or gas temperature — could be exceeded in any of the normal and likely emergency conditions within the flight envelope declared by the engine constructor, establish to the satisfaction of the Agency that the most severe conditions likely to occur will have no unacceptable effects on the engine. | Required |

CS-E 700 is a single unnumbered sentence, so the `Ref` cell carries no sub-point
identifier.

The paragraph works backwards from the envelope to the tests. Every condition
that some other Subpart E paragraph substantiated is checked against what the
declared flight envelope can produce, in normal **and** in likely emergency
conditions. Where the envelope can exceed the substantiated value, the excess
must be shown acceptable.

The examples are introduced by "e.g.", so they do not bound the conditions in
scope. The source names three — "air or gas pressure, thrust, gas temperature"
[CS-E 700] — of which the thrust example has no turboshaft case.

"Likely emergency conditions" is the phrase that gives the paragraph reach. A
condition need not arise in normal operation to be covered.

## Compliance

- Survey of the operating conditions substantiated by the other Subpart E paragraphs, with the value substantiated for each [CS-E 700].
- Comparison of each against the most severe value the declared flight envelope can produce, in normal and likely emergency conditions [CS-E 700].
- Demonstration that the most severe conditions likely to occur have no unacceptable effects on the engine, established to the satisfaction of the Agency [CS-E 700].
- Declared flight envelope, as the reference the comparison is made against [CS-E 700], consistent with the assumptions of [[CS-E 30]] and the limitations of [[CS-E 40|CS-E 40(d)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies. Nothing in it is restricted by engine type or rating.

CS-E 700 points to "(See AMC E 700)". That AMC is titled "Excess Operating
Conditions (Turbine Engines for Aeroplanes)" and is aeroplane-only by its own
title, so it is excluded from this vault and has no note. CS-E 700 therefore has
no applicable accepted means for this engine, and the demonstration rests on the
specification and on agreement with the Agency. The cross-reference is rendered as
plain text here for that reason.

The OEI ratings widen what the envelope can produce. Operation at 30-Second OEI
and 2-Minute OEI is itself an emergency condition within the declared envelope,
and the conditions those ratings generate — gas temperature in particular — are
the clearest case for this paragraph. The related over-limit paragraphs are
[[CS-E 820]] for over-torque, [[CS-E 830]] for over-speed, [[CS-E 860]] for
turbine rotor over-temperature and [[CS-E 870]] for exhaust gas
over-temperature, and [[CS-E 920]] is the over-temperature test.

CS-E 700 also reaches the pressure work of [[CS-E 640]]. The maximum possible
pressure that [[AMC E 640|AMC E 640(1)]] defines already includes the most adverse
combination of operational conditions together with Failures more likely than
Extremely Remote, so the two paragraphs approach the same question from different
directions.

## Not applicable

- **The "thrust" example** in the CS-E 700 list of operating conditions — one of three items in an "e.g." list. This engine produces shaft power; the air or gas pressure and gas temperature examples are kept, and the list is open in any case.
