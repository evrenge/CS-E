# CS-E 600 — Tests - General

> [!summary]
> This paragraph fixes the hardware and the configuration common to every
> Subpart E test. Air intakes must be representative of the powerplant intakes,
> and jet pipes and propelling nozzles must be representative too. Optional air
> bleed is closed unless a test requires otherwise. Internal cleaning during the
> endurance test is allowed on agreed terms. For a rotorcraft engine, tests are
> normally made with the engine mounted in its installed attitude.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Make all tests with air intakes conforming to an acceptable design that is as representative of the powerplant intakes as is practicable. | Required |
| **(b)** | Make all tests with acceptable representative jet pipes and propelling nozzles, except as permitted under CS-E 740(f)(4)(i). | Required |
| **(b)** | The approval of other jet pipes and / or propelling nozzles for particular installations will be considered individually as necessary. | Statement |
| **(c)** | Close any optional air bleed during all relevant tests, unless otherwise required for specific tests. | Required |
| **(d)** | Where dirt accumulates within the engine due to the test house environment, it will be acceptable to clean the engine internally at agreed intervals during the endurance test of CS-E 740, by an agreed method which does not involve stripping any part of the engine or necessitate removal of the engine from the test bed. | Permitted |
| **(e)** | Engines for Rotorcraft. Make all tests normally with the engine mounted in the attitude in which it will be installed. | Required |

Sub-point (e) is written for this engine type by its own heading, "Engines for
Rotorcraft" [CS-E 600(e)]. The word "normally" admits a departure, and
[[AMC E 600|AMC E 600(e)]] states the price: any difference between the test
attitude and the attitude in the intended rotorcraft installations must be
justified.

The cleaning relief in (d) is bounded on three sides. The intervals must be
agreed, the method must be agreed, and the method must neither strip any part nor
require the engine to leave the test bed. Anything beyond that is a repair, which
[[CS-E 150|CS-E 150(b)]] permits only against agreed penalty testing.

Sub-point (c) closes optional bleed by default. That aligns the test with the
rating basis of [[CS-E 40|CS-E 40(c)]], which establishes ratings without aircraft
bleed.

## Compliance

- Air intake design statement showing representativeness of the powerplant intakes [CS-E 600(a)].
- Jet pipe and propelling nozzle configuration statement for each test [CS-E 600(b)].
- Bleed configuration recorded per test, with justification where optional bleed was open [CS-E 600(c)].
- Agreed internal cleaning method and intervals, where cleaning is used during the endurance test of [[CS-E 740]] [CS-E 600(d)].
- Engine attitude recorded for each test, with justification of any difference from the intended rotorcraft installation attitude [CS-E 600(e)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 600]].

## Application to this engine

All sub-points apply, and (e) is the one written specifically for a rotorcraft
engine.

Attitude is not a formality here. Testing in the installed attitude affects oil
pick-up, scavenge and venting, which is why [[CS-E 570|CS-E 570(a)(1)]] requires
proper oil system functioning "under all intended flight attitudes" and why
[[CS-E 680]] tests inclination and gyroscopic load effects. Because the
rotorcraft installation is not fixed, the installed attitude is an assumption
under [[CS-E 30]].

[VERIFY: the engine installation attitude in the intended rotorcraft is not
recorded in `engine_profile.md`. CS-E 600(e) makes it the normal test attitude,
and AMC E 600(e) requires any difference to be justified.]

Sub-point (b) refers to jet pipes and propelling nozzles. A turboshaft delivers
shaft power rather than thrust, but it still has an exhaust system, and the
sub-point governs its representativeness in test. The exception it names,
CS-E 740(f)(4)(i), is part of the endurance test paragraph.

CS-E 600 sets the configuration; [[CS-E 140]] sets the engine configuration rules
that apply to every test in CS-E, and [[CS-E 150]] sets how the tests are
conducted. The three are read together.
