# CS-E 120 — Identification

> [!summary]
> Engine identification follows Part 21 rather than CS-E itself, so the
> substance of sub-point (a) sits outside this specification. CS-E adds one
> requirement of its own: major engine modules that can be changed independently
> in service must be identified so that parts stay traceable and module
> interchangeability between engine variants stays under control.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Comply with points 21.A.801(a) and (b), and point 21.A.805 of Part 21, for engine identification. | Required |
| **(b)** | Suitably identify major engine modules that can be changed independently in service, so as to ensure traceability of parts and to enable proper control over the interchangeability of such modules with different engine variants. | Required |

Sub-point (a) carries no CS-E content of its own; it points to Part 21, the EU
regulation that contains points 21.A.801 and 21.A.805 [CS-E 120(a)]. Sub-point
(b) is where CS-E adds a specification, and it applies only to **major** modules
that are **independently changeable in service** [CS-E 120(b)]. A module that
cannot be changed independently in service falls outside (b).

Two purposes are stated and they are not the same: traceability of parts, and
control over interchangeability with different engine variants [CS-E 120(b)].
An identification scheme that achieves the first but lets an incompatible module
be fitted to another variant does not satisfy (b).

## Compliance

- Engine identification scheme meeting the Part 21 points named in (a) [CS-E 120(a)].
- Module identification scheme for major, independently changeable modules, showing how it supports parts traceability and interchangeability control across engine variants [CS-E 120(b)].
- Definition of the engine variants across which module interchangeability is controlled [CS-E 120(b)].

## Application to this engine

Both sub-points apply; neither is restricted by engine type, rating or control
system.

CS-E 120 has no AMC of its own. The related marking and record-keeping
specifications are in [[CS-E 110]]. That paragraph requires each part to be
identifiable with its drawing [CS-E 110(b)]. It also requires retained records
establishing the manufacturing history of Engine Critical Parts and other
Agency-designated parts [CS-E 110(c)]. CS-E 120(b) works at module level rather
than part level, so the two schemes are complementary: CS-E 110 traces a part to
its drawing, CS-E 120 traces a module through service and controls where it may
be fitted.

**What sub-point (a) points to.** Part 21 is now held, so the pointer can be
followed. Point 21.A.801(a) lists what the identification must include: the
manufacturer's name, the product designation, the manufacturer's serial number,
the "'EXEMPT' mark for engines, when the competent authority has granted an
exemption from the applicable environmental protection requirements", and any
other information the Agency finds appropriate [ext 21.A.801(a)].

Point 21.A.801(b) fixes how, and it places the duty on the engine manufacturer.
The engine is identified "by means of a fireproof plate" carrying that
information, marked "by etching, stamping, engraving, or other approved method
of fireproof marking". Part 21 requires the plate to be secured so that it is
accessible and legible, and "will not likely be defaced or removed during normal
service, or lost or destroyed in an accident" [ext 21.A.801(b)].

Part 21 does not define "fireproof". The definition this vault records is
CS-Definitions', read in [[CS-E 130]]: the capability to withstand heat applied
by a flame for 15 minutes without a failure that creates a hazard to the
aircraft [ext CS-Definitions, Fireproof].

Point 21.A.805 adds the critical part duty. Each manufacturer of a part fitted
on a type-certificated product and identified as a critical part "shall
permanently and legibly mark that part with a part number and a serial number"
[ext 21.A.805]. Its guidance material makes the scope a design decision: "a part
that requires individual traceability for the management of its continued
airworthiness, as identified by the design approval holder, shall be permanently
marked with a part number and a serial number" [ext GM1 21.A.805].

That guidance then cites CS-E back. It gives [[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(c)]] as an
example of a certification specification that identifies parts to be marked, and
that sub-point names Engine Critical Parts and refers to [[CS-E 515]]
[ext GM1 21.A.805]. The loop closes: CS-E 515 identifies the Engine Critical
Parts, CS-E 110(c) requires them to be marked with records maintained, and
point 21.A.805 requires the individual part number and serial number.

The 'EXEMPT' mark is the one item with no CS-E counterpart. It belongs to the
environmental protection route recorded in [[CS 34.2]], and it arises only where
an exemption has been granted.

## Amendment history

Amended at Amendment 7. The change is a citation correction, not a change of
obligation:

- **Before:** "The Engine identification must comply with 21.A.801(a) and (b), and 21.A.805"
- **After:** "The Engine identification must comply with points 21.A.801(a) and (b), and point 21.A.805 of Part 21"

Amendment 7 inserted "points", "point" and "of Part 21". The cited provisions are
unchanged, so the duty is unchanged. The same amendment made the same kind of
correction to [[CS-E 160|CS-E 160(a)]], though there the cited provision itself
changed. The paragraph carries `[Amdt No: E/7]`.
