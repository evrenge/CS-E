# CS-E 525 — Continued Rotation

> [!summary]
> If a main rotating system keeps turning after an in-flight shutdown and no
> means is provided to stop it, that rotation must not produce effects that would
> be unacceptable under CS-E 510. The test period is not a fixed duration. It is
> the maximum period of flight in the flight conditions expected with that
> engine inoperative. The demonstration therefore depends on how long the
> aircraft can be expected to fly with the engine shut down.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Where any of the engine's main rotating systems will continue to rotate after the engine is shut down for any reason while in flight, and means to prevent that continued rotation are not provided, ensure that any continued rotation during the maximum period of flight, and in the flight conditions expected to occur with that engine inoperative, does not result in effects that would be unacceptable under CS-E 510. | Required |

CS-E 525 is a single unnumbered sentence, so the `Ref` cell carries no sub-point
identifier.

The specification is conditional on two things at once: a main rotating system
that continues to rotate, **and** the absence of means to prevent it. Providing
such means removes the obligation; without them, the rotation must be shown
acceptable.

"Unacceptable under CS-E 510" is the acceptance criterion, so the standard is not
fixed here. It resolves to the Failure classification and probability targets of
[[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(g)]] and [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(3)]].

The duration is likewise not fixed. It is "the maximum period of flight … in the
flight conditions expected to occur with that Engine inoperative"
[CS-E 525], which is an installation-dependent quantity and therefore an
assumption under [[CS-E 30]].

## Compliance

- Identification of the main rotating systems that continue to rotate after in-flight shutdown, and of any means provided to prevent it [CS-E 525].
- Test or analysis demonstrating that continued rotation produces no effects unacceptable under [[CS-E 510]], taking account of the conditions imposed by a typical aircraft installation [CS-E 525]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 525|AMC E 525(1)]].
- Statement of the maximum period of flight with the engine inoperative, and the flight conditions expected during it [CS-E 525]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 525|AMC E 525(2)]], declared under [[CS-E 30]].
- Assessment of complete loss of engine oil, and of rotor unbalance resulting from blade loss and subsequent rotor damage, over extended periods of continued rotation [CS-E 525]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 525|AMC E 525(3)]].
- Engine-to-aircraft interface conditions from rotor unbalance and rotational speed during continued rotation after blade loss, determined under [[CS-E 520|CS-E 520(c)(2)]] and provided in the installation documents of [[CS-E 20]] [CS-E 525]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 525|AMC E 525(4)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies, and [[AMC E 525]] addresses this engine type directly.

[[AMC E 525|AMC E 525(1)]] names the two mechanisms: "Continued rotation can be
either due to windmilling or due to mechanical effects such as clutch drag in the
case of a multi-engined rotorcraft". The clutch drag case is specific to a
multi-engine rotorcraft installation, which is the application declared in
`engine_profile.md`. A turboshaft in a multi-engine rotorcraft is therefore
exposed to a driven-rotation mechanism that a windmilling analysis alone does not
cover. The source does not name the component whose drag produces it, nor the
means that would prevent it: CS-E 525 says only "means to prevent that continued
rotation", and AMC E 525(1) says only "mechanical effects such as clutch drag".

The two conditions named in [[AMC E 525|AMC E 525(3)]] both connect elsewhere in
the vault. Complete loss of engine oil is the case that
[[AMC E 130|AMC E 130(2)(c)]] refers to. It notes that oil system components of
turbine engines may keep flowing oil after shutdown, because of continued
rotation. That is why those components are usually evaluated to a Fireproof
rather than a Fire Resistant standard. Rotor unbalance from blade loss is the
output of [[CS-E 810]], fed through [[CS-E 520|CS-E 520(c)(2)]].

The continued rotation duration is set by how long the rotorcraft flies with one
engine shut down. That is precisely the flight phase the declared OEI ratings
exist to cover. Continuous OEI operation on the remaining engine is the
condition under which the shut-down engine keeps rotating.

[VERIFY: the maximum period of flight expected with one engine inoperative is not
recorded in `engine_profile.md`. It sets the duration for the continued rotation
demonstration and is an installation assumption under CS-E 30.]
