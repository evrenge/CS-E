# CS-E 515 — Engine Critical Parts

> [!summary]
> The integrity of the Engine Critical Parts identified by the safety analysis
> must be established through three plans, not one. The Engineering Plan fixes
> an Approved Life at which the part is withdrawn before Hazardous Engine
> Effects can occur, supported by Damage Tolerance assessments. The
> Manufacturing Plan identifies the constraints needed to produce parts with the
> Attributes the Engineering Plan assumed. The Service Management Plan defines
> the in-service maintenance and repair processes that keep those Attributes,
> and becomes part of the instructions for continued airworthiness.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Establish the integrity of the Engine Critical Parts identified under CS-E 510 by the Engineering Plan, Manufacturing Plan and Service Management Plan of (a), (b) and (c). | Required |
| **(a)** | Provide an Engineering Plan whose execution establishes and maintains that the combinations of loads, material properties, environmental influences and operating conditions, including the effects of parts influencing these parameters, are sufficiently well known or predictable — by validated analysis, test or service experience — to allow each Engine Critical Part to be withdrawn from service at an Approved Life before Hazardous Engine Effects can occur. | Required |
| **(a)** | Perform appropriate Damage Tolerance assessments to address the potential for Failure from material, manufacturing and service-induced anomalies within the Approved Life of the part. | Required |
| **(a)** | Publish the Approved Life as required in CS-E 25(b). | Required |
| **(b)** | Provide a Manufacturing Plan which identifies the specific manufacturing constraints necessary to consistently produce Engine Critical Parts with the Attributes required by the Engineering Plan. | Required |
| **(c)** | Provide a Service Management Plan which defines in-service processes for maintenance and repair of Engine Critical Parts which will maintain Attributes consistent with those required by the Engineering Plan. | Required |
| **(c)** | Make those processes part of the instructions for continued airworthiness. | Required |

An Engine Critical Part is a part that relies on meeting the integrity
specifications of CS-E 515 to avoid its Primary Failure, which is likely to
result in a Hazardous Engine Effect [CS-E 15]. An Approved Life is the mandatory
replacement life of a part, approved by the Agency. Attributes are the inherent
characteristics of a finished part that determine its capability [CS-E 15].

The opening sentence is a single obligation with three named means, and the
three are cumulative [CS-E 515]. The paragraph does not offer a choice among the
plans.

The three plans form a closed loop. [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(1)]] describes them as
"a closed-loop system which link the assumptions made in the Engineering Plan to
how the part is manufactured and maintained in service". The Engineering Plan
makes assumptions; the other two exist to keep those assumptions true in the
factory and in service.

Which parts CS-E 515 reaches is not decided here. The population comes from
[[CS-E 510]]. [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(a)(2)]] requires every Engine Critical
Part to be clearly identified in the summary. [[CS-E 510|CS-E 510(c)]] makes
CS-E 515 the route to Extremely Remote for single elements whose Failure
probability cannot be sensibly quantified.

The Approved Life is not merely recorded; it is published as an airworthiness
limitation under [[CS-E 25|CS-E 25(b)]] [CS-E 515(a)].

## Compliance

- Engineering Plan covering loads, material properties, environmental influences and operating conditions, with the influencing parts, validated by analysis, test or service experience [CS-E 515(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(3)]].
- Approved Life determination for each Engine Critical Part [CS-E 515(a)], published in the airworthiness limitations section under [[CS-E 25|CS-E 25(b)]].
- Damage Tolerance assessment addressing material, manufacturing and service-induced anomalies [CS-E 515(a)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(3)(d)(v)]].
- Manufacturing Plan identifying the constraints that deliver the required Attributes [CS-E 515(b)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(4)]].
- Service Management Plan defining in-service maintenance and repair processes, incorporated into the instructions for continued airworthiness [CS-E 515(c)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(5)]].
- Serviceable and repairable damage limits published in the instructions for continued airworthiness [CS-E 515(c)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 515|AMC E 515(3)(d)(v)]].
- Marking and manufacturing-history records for Engine Critical Parts, required by [[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(c)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies in full. Nothing in it is restricted by engine type, and
the population of parts it governs is set by the safety analysis rather than by
the paragraph.

Two provisions of [[AMC E 515]] are written for this engine specifically. For
rotorcraft turbine engines, the AMC states that "the representative usage of the
30-minute Power rating" should be considered in the Engine Flight Cycle used to
establish the Approved Life [AMC E 515(3)(d)(i)], and `engine_profile.md`
declares Rated 30-Minute Power under [[CS-E 40|CS-E 40(b)(4)]]. Separately, for
engines with OEI ratings the airworthiness limitations section should include a
method for accounting for cycles used at the OEI ratings [AMC E 515(6)(b)]. This
engine declares 30-Second OEI, 2-Minute OEI and Continuous OEI, so both
provisions apply.

CS-E 515 is the paragraph the rest of the vault leans on for rotating part
integrity. [[AMC E 510|AMC E 510(3)(d)(iii)]] states that for major rotating
parts the Extremely Remote objective "can only be ensured through compliance
with CS-E 515, supplemented by CS-E 840 and CS-E 850". The same AMC is equally
clear about the limit: CS-E 515 does **not** provide a valid basis for an
Extremely Remote blade Failure probability, because it does not address every
factor affecting blade reliability, foreign object damage among them
[AMC E 510(3)(d)(iii)].

CS-E 515 also supplies work to other paragraphs. The residual stress assessment
for forged Engine Critical Parts is in [[AMC E 70|AMC E 70(2)(a)]]. The residual
stress consideration itself is required by [[CS-E 100|CS-E 100(a)]]. The
pressure cycling item in [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(2)(c)]] cites AMC E 515(3)(e).
