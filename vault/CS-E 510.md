# CS-E 510 — Safety Analysis

> [!summary]
> This paragraph requires an analysis of the engine, including its control
> system, assessing the likely consequence of every Failure that can reasonably
> be expected. It fixes the three Failure classes used throughout CS-E — Minor,
> Major and Hazardous Engine Effects — and sets numerical targets for the last
> two. It also governs what the analysis may rely on: where it depends on
> maintenance intervals, pre-flight checks, extra instrumentation or flight crew
> action, each must be identified and substantiated, and published where it
> affects continued airworthiness.

## Requirement

### The analysis and its targets

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)(1)** | Carry out an analysis of the engine, including the control system, to assess the likely consequence of all Failures that can reasonably be expected to occur. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(i)** | Take account of aircraft-level devices and procedures assumed to be associated with a typical installation, and state those assumptions in the analysis. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(ii)** | Take account of consequential secondary Failures and dormant Failures. | Required |
| **(a)(1)(iii)** | Take account of multiple Failures referred to in CS-E 510(d), or that result in the Hazardous Engine Effects defined in CS-E 510(g)(2). | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Make a summary of those Failures that could result in Major Engine Effects or Hazardous Engine Effects as defined in CS-E 510(g), together with an estimate of the probability of occurrence of those effects. | Required |
| **(a)(2)** | Clearly identify any Engine Critical Part in that summary. | Required |
| **(a)(3)** | Show that Hazardous Engine Effects are predicted to occur at a rate not in excess of that defined as Extremely Remote — probability less than 10⁻⁷ per Engine flight hour. | Required |
| **(a)(3)** | For engine certification, it is acceptable to consider the intent achieved if the probability of a Hazardous Engine Effect arising from an individual Failure can be predicted to be not greater than 10⁻⁸ per Engine flight hour. | Statement |
| **(a)(4)** | Show that Major Engine Effects are predicted to occur at a rate not in excess of that defined as Remote — probability less than 10⁻⁵ per Engine flight hour. | Required |
| **(b)** | If significant doubt exists as to the effects of Failures and likely combination of Failures, any assumption may be required to be verified by test. | Statement |
| **(c)** | Where the probability of Primary Failures of certain single elements cannot be sensibly estimated in numerical terms, and their Failure is likely to result in Hazardous Engine Effects, place reliance on meeting the prescribed integrity specifications of CS-E 515 to support the objective of an Extremely Remote probability of Failure. | Required |
| **(c)** | State these instances in the safety analysis as required in CS-E 510(a)(2). | Required |
| **(d)** | Where reliance is placed on a safety system to prevent a Failure progressing to Hazardous Engine Effects, include in the analysis the possibility of a safety system Failure in combination with a basic engine Failure. | Required |
| **(d)** | Where items of a safety system are outside the control of the applicant, clearly state the safety analysis assumptions about their reliability and identify them in accordance with CS-E 30. | Required |

The two probability figures in (a)(3) are not alternatives. 10⁻⁷ per engine
flight hour is the target for Hazardous Engine Effects in total. 10⁻⁸ per engine
flight hour is the per-Failure figure that CS-E accepts as achieving the intent,
because individual estimates are often too imprecise to sum meaningfully.

Sub-point (c) is the bridge to [[CS-E 515]]. Where a single element's Primary
Failure probability cannot be sensibly quantified, the numerical route is closed
and the integrity route of CS-E 515 replaces it. The paragraph requires this to
be visible: such instances must be stated in the summary.

A "safety system" under (d) is broadly defined and includes procedures, not only
hardware: "safety devices, instrumentation, early warning devices, maintenance
checks, and other similar equipment or procedures" [CS-E 510(d)].

### What the analysis may depend on

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(e)** | Where the acceptability of the safety analysis depends on any of the following items, identify them in the analysis and appropriately substantiate them. | Required |
| **(e)(1)** | Maintenance actions carried out at stated intervals, including verification of the serviceability of items which could fail in a dormant manner. | Required |
| **(e)(1)** | Where necessary to prevent Hazardous Engine Effects occurring at a rate in excess of Extremely Remote, publish the maintenance intervals in the airworthiness limitations section of the instructions for continued airworthiness required under CS-E 25. | Required |
| **(e)(1)** | Where errors in maintenance of the engine, including the Engine Control System, could lead to Hazardous Engine Effects, include appropriate procedures in the relevant engine manuals. | Required |
| **(e)(2)** | Verification of the satisfactory functioning of safety or other devices at pre-flight or other stated periods, with the details published in the appropriate manual. | Required |
| **(e)(3)** | The provision of specific instrumentation not otherwise required. | Required |
| **(e)(4)** | Flight crew actions, identified in the operating instructions required under CS-E 20(d). | Required |

### Systems the analysis must investigate

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(f)** | If applicable, the safety analysis must also consider, but not be limited to, investigation of the systems listed in (f)(1) to (f)(9). | Required |
| **(f)(1)** | Indicating equipment. | Required |
| **(f)(2)** | Aircraft-supplied data or electrical power. | Required |
| **(f)(3)** | Compressor bleed systems. | Required |
| **(f)(5)** | Gas temperature control systems. | Required |
| **(f)(6)** | Engine speed, power or thrust governors and fuel control systems. | Required |
| **(f)(7)** | Engine over-speed, over-temperature or topping limiters. | Required |

The list in (f) is explicitly open: the analysis "must also consider, but not be
limited to" these items [CS-E 510(f)], and each applies only "If applicable".

### Failure definitions

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(g)(1)** | An engine Failure whose only consequence is partial or complete loss of thrust or power, and associated engine services, from the engine must be regarded as a Minor Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(2)(i)** | Non-containment of high-energy debris is a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(2)(ii)** | Concentration of toxic products in the engine bleed air for the cabin sufficient to incapacitate crew or passengers is a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(2)(iii)** | Significant thrust in the opposite direction to that commanded by the pilot is a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(2)(iv)** | Uncontrolled fire is a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(2)(v)** | Failure of the engine mount system leading to inadvertent engine separation is a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(2)(vii)** | Complete inability to shut the engine down is a Hazardous Engine Effect. | Statement |
| **(g)(3)** | An effect falling between those covered in CS-E 510(g)(1) and (2) must be regarded as a Major Engine Effect. | Statement |

The definitions in (g) are the reference point for the whole of CS-E. Hazardous
Engine Effect appears in [[CS-E 80|CS-E 80(a)(1)(ii)]],
[[CS-E 110|CS-E 110(d)]], [[CS-E 130|CS-E 130(a)]], [[CS-E 515]] and many
Subpart E paragraphs, and every one of them resolves here.

## Compliance

- Failure analysis of the engine including the control system, covering all reasonably expected Failures, consequential secondary Failures and dormant Failures [CS-E 510(a)(1)]. Accepted means: [[AMC E 510]].
- Statement of the aircraft-level devices and procedures assumed for a typical installation [CS-E 510(a)(1)(i)], consistent with the assumptions declared under [[CS-E 30]].
- Summary of Failures leading to Major or Hazardous Engine Effects, with probability estimates and Engine Critical Parts clearly identified [CS-E 510(a)(2)].
- Quantitative demonstration against 10⁻⁷ per Engine flight hour for Hazardous Engine Effects and 10⁻⁵ per Engine flight hour for Major Engine Effects, with the 10⁻⁸ per-Failure figure where individual estimates are imprecise [CS-E 510(a)(3)], [CS-E 510(a)(4)].
- Identification of single elements whose Primary Failure probability cannot be sensibly quantified, handled through the integrity specifications of [[CS-E 515]] [CS-E 510(c)].
- Safety system analysis including combined safety system and basic engine Failure, with reliability assumptions stated for items outside the applicant's control [CS-E 510(d)].
- Substantiation of every dependency under (e), with the maintenance intervals published in the airworthiness limitations section of the instructions for continued airworthiness required by [[CS-E 25]] [CS-E 510(e)(1)].
- Maintenance error procedures in the relevant engine manuals, where such errors could lead to Hazardous Engine Effects [CS-E 510(e)(1)].
- Pre-flight or periodic verification details published in the appropriate manual [CS-E 510(e)(2)].
- Flight crew actions identified in the operating instructions required by [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]] [CS-E 510(e)(4)].
- Investigation of each applicable system in the (f) list [CS-E 510(f)].
- Test verification of any assumption where significant doubt exists [CS-E 510(b)].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies, less the sub-points recorded below.

Sub-point (f)(2), aircraft-supplied data or electrical power, is load-bearing for
this engine. A full-authority EECS depends on the aircraft for power and data, so
the analysis must investigate those inputs, and
[[CS-E 50|CS-E 50(h)]] carries the matching control system specification. Those
items sit outside the applicant's control, so (d) also requires their reliability
assumptions to be stated and identified under [[CS-E 30]].

Sub-point (f)(7), over-speed, over-temperature and topping limiters, connects to
[[CS-E 170]]. [[AMC E 170]] names an over-speed protection system or torque
limiter as unlikely to be exercised by the scheduled tests of [[CS-E 740]]. The
analysis and the verification testing therefore meet here.

The OEI ratings create specific dormant Failure work. [[AMC E 20|AMC E 20(f)(2)]]
states that the safety analysis of CS-E 510 "should consider dormant Failures
which could lead to non-availability of the OEI ratings", and that the results
form part of the data required under [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(f)]]. This engine declares
30-Second OEI, 2-Minute OEI and Continuous OEI, so that consideration is live.

Sub-point (e)(1) is the route by which the safety analysis produces airworthiness
limitations. Any maintenance interval the analysis relies on to keep Hazardous
Engine Effects Extremely Remote becomes a published limitation under
[[CS-E 25]], not merely a recommendation.

Where the rotorcraft credits an engine torque limiting device in its
transmission tests [ext CS 29.927(b)(2)], [ext CS 27.927(b)(2)], the failure of
that limiter is an engine-side Failure condition. Its effects are assessed here
and under [[CS-E 50|CS-E 50(d)]]. Both rotorcraft codes carry the same
assumption, so the duty does not depend on which one the installation targets.
See [[CS 29.927]] and [[CS 27.927]].

The recording and retrieval system for OEI usage is assessed here too:
[[AMC E 60|AMC E 60(d)(3)]] derives its development assurance level from the
system safety analysis required under [[CS-E 50|CS-E 50(d)]].

## Not applicable

- **(f)(4)** — refrigerant injection systems. `engine_profile.md` declares no refrigerant injection, so the item does not arise.
- **(f)(8)**, **(f)(9)** — propeller control systems, and engine or propeller thrust reversal systems. A turboshaft driving a rotorcraft transmission has no propeller, and propeller and thrust reverser material is excluded from this vault by scope.
- **(g)(2)(vi)** — release of the propeller by the engine, qualified in the source by "if applicable". Not applicable for the same reason.
