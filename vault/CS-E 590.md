# CS-E 590 — Starter Systems

> [!summary]
> Where the starter is declared as part of the engine, its design and that of
> its drive mechanism must prevent starter over-speeding to an extent that could
> cause a Hazardous Engine Effect, under any engine Fault condition that is not
> Extremely Remote. The possibility of the starter staying connected, or
> reconnecting after a drive system Failure, must be considered. If the argument
> depends on safety provisions supplied by the installation, that need must be
> declared.

## Requirement

| Ref | Obligation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| — | Where the starter is declared as part of the engine, design the starter and its associated drive mechanism so that over-speeding of the starter, to an extent which could result in a Hazardous Engine Effect, cannot occur under any Fault conditions in the engine which cannot be classified as Extremely Remote. | Required |
| — | Consider the possibility of the starter remaining connected, or subsequently becoming reconnected, to the engine resulting from any Failure of the drive system. | Required |
| — | Where compliance depends on safety provisions to be provided as part of the installation, declare the need for such provisions. | Required |

CS-E 590 is a single unnumbered paragraph of three sentences, so the `Ref` cells
carry no sub-point identifier.

The trigger is conditional: the paragraph binds "Where the starter is declared
as part of the Engine" [CS-E 590]. A starter supplied by the aircraft falls
outside it, and is then equipment under [[CS-E 80|CS-E 80(c)]].

The Fault population is defined by exclusion. Every engine Fault condition that
cannot be classified as Extremely Remote is in scope [CS-E 590]. The
classification therefore comes from the safety analysis of [[CS-E 510]], rather
than from a separate judgement.

The second sentence covers a distinct mechanism from the first. Over-speeding is
one path; remaining connected or reconnecting after a drive Failure is another,
and it must be considered in its own right [CS-E 590].

The third sentence permits reliance on the installation, on one condition.
Installation-supplied safety provisions may carry part of the argument, but the
need for them must be declared [CS-E 590]. This makes them an assumption under
[[CS-E 30|CS-E 30(b)]] and an item for the instructions for installation of
[[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].

## Compliance

- Statement of whether the starter is declared as part of the engine [CS-E 590].
- Starter and drive mechanism design substantiation against over-speeding to a Hazardous Engine Effect, across all engine Fault conditions not classified as Extremely Remote [CS-E 590], using the classification from [[CS-E 510]].
- Assessment of the starter remaining connected, or reconnecting, following any drive system Failure [CS-E 590].
- Declaration of any installation-supplied safety provisions relied on [CS-E 590], carried into [[CS-E 30]] and the instructions for installation of [[CS-E 20|CS-E 20(d)]].

## Application to this engine

The paragraph applies wherever the starter is declared as part of the engine. It
has no AMC of its own.

CS-E 590 is cited from two other places in the vault. [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(4)]]
lists, among the specifications applicable to equipment with high-energy rotors,
the "Establishment that drive mechanism will prevent the Engine driving the
starter to a dangerous speed, unless such a probability is Extremely Remote (see
CS-E 590)". A turbine starter supplied with air or gas from an external source
is precisely the worked example of [[AMC E 80|AMC E 80(4)]]. CS-E 590 and the
containment categories of that AMC therefore address the same hardware from two
directions. CS-E 590 prevents the over-speed; [[CS-E 80|CS-E 80(d)]] contains
the consequences if it occurs.

[[CS-E 140|CS-E 140(e)]] names CS-E 590 among the paragraphs whose features may
be incorporated as part of the aircraft installation rather than the engine type
design. Where that is the case and engine performance is affected, the feature
must still be satisfactorily represented throughout the engine tests.

[VERIFY: whether the starter is declared as part of the engine type design.
`engine_profile.md` does not record this. If it is not, CS-E 590 does not bind
and the starter is treated as equipment under CS-E 80(c) and CS-E 20(c).]
